# 🎹 LEVEL 1 — KEYBOARD GEOGRAPHY

## Bucket 1.7 — Visual → Tactile Mapping

### 🎯 Main Goal

By the end of this bucket, you should be able to hear/think:

> **“Find F.”**

and your hand should naturally move toward the correct F **without your eyes continuously tracking the keyboard.**

---

# 1.7.1 — 👀 Look → Touch

### What you are training

**Visual information → Hand movement**

At this stage, you are allowed to look at the keyboard.

### How to practice

1. Sit comfortably at the keyboard.
2. Pick a random note.

   * C
   * D
   * E
   * F
   * G
   * A
   * B
3. Look carefully at that note.
4. Move your finger toward it.
5. Touch the key.
6. Say the note name aloud.
7. Release.

### Example

You choose:

> **G**

Look at the keyboard → locate G → move your finger → touch G.

Then:

> **A**

Look → locate A → touch A.

### Practice pattern

Do **20 repetitions**.

Example:

```text
C → Touch
G → Touch
D → Touch
A → Touch
F → Touch
B → Touch
E → Touch
C → Touch
...
```

### Important rule

Don't rush.

At this stage your goal isn't speed.

Your goal is:

> **“When I see the note, my hand knows exactly where to go.”**

### Progression

**Stage 1:** Look → slowly touch
**Stage 2:** Look → quickly touch
**Stage 3:** Look → touch without hesitation

### 🎯 Success condition

You should be able to perform **20 random notes with ~95% accuracy**.

---

# 1.7.2 — ✋ Touch → Identify

Now we reverse the direction.

Previously:

> 👀 Note → ✋ Touch

Now:

> ✋ Touch → 🧠 Identify

This develops your **tactile awareness**.

### How to practice

1. Look at the keyboard.
2. Choose a key.
3. Touch it with one finger.
4. Don't press yet.
5. Identify the note.
6. Say the note aloud.
7. Then look and verify.

### Example

Touch a key.

Your brain says:

> “That's F.”

Look down.

Correct! ✅

Next:

> “That's A.”

Check.

Correct! ✅

### Important variation

Don't always touch white keys in the same order.

Mix them randomly.

```text
E → A → C → G → B → D → F
```

### Next level

Touch a key **without immediately looking at it**.

Ask yourself:

> “What note am I touching?”

Then look.

### 🎯 Success condition

Get **20 consecutive identifications with at least 18 correct**.

---

# 1.7.3 — 👀➡️🙈 Look Away → Reach

Now things become more interesting. 😂

This is where you begin developing **spatial memory**.

### What you're training

> 👀 See target
> ↓
> 🧠 Remember location
> ↓
> 🙈 Look away
> ↓
> ✋ Reach

### How to practice

Suppose your target is:

> **E**

1. Look at the keyboard.
2. Locate E.
3. Keep your eyes on E for about **1 second**.
4. Look away.
5. Move your finger toward E.
6. Touch the key.
7. Look back.
8. Check accuracy.

### Example

```text
Target: E

LOOK 👀
   ↓
Remember location 🧠
   ↓
LOOK AWAY 🙈
   ↓
REACH ✋
   ↓
TOUCH 🎹
   ↓
CHECK 👀
```

### Start with nearby targets

Initially use:

```text
C
D
E
F
G
A
B
```

Then make it harder by selecting notes farther away.

For example:

> See C → look away → reach for A.

### 🎯 Success condition

Perform **20 attempts with at least 17/20 correct**.

# 1.7.4 — 🧭 Landmark → Reach

This is one of the **most important exercises in the entire bucket.**

You're going to use the black keys as your built-in keyboard map.

Remember:

```text
      ♯ ♯       ♯ ♯ ♯
      │ │       │ │ │
C D E F G A B C
```

The black-key pattern tells you where everything is.

### Your two major landmarks

## C

C is immediately to the **left of the group of 2 black keys**.

```text
   ● ●
   ↑ ↑
   C
```

## F

F is immediately to the **left of the group of 3 black keys**.

```text
   ● ● ●
   ↑ ↑ ↑
   F
```

### Exercise

Pick:

> **C**

Look briefly.

Find the pair of black keys.

Identify C.

Then:

> Look away → reach C.

Next:

> **F**

Find the group of three black keys.

Identify F.

Look away.

Reach F.

### Now make it harder

Use the landmark to locate neighboring notes.

For example:

> Find C → reach D

Because D is between the two black keys.

Or:

> Find F → reach G

Because G is between the first and second black keys of the group of three.

### Mental map

```text
2 BLACK KEYS

       ● ●
       ↑ ↑
       C D E
       ↑ ↑ ↑
      C-landmark


3 BLACK KEYS

       ● ● ●
       ↑ ↑ ↑
       F G A B
       ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑
      F-landmark
```

### 🎯 Success condition

You should eventually be able to:

> See the black-key pattern → mentally identify C/F → reach the target with minimal visual checking.

# 1.7.5 — 🎯 Random Blind Reach

Now we start making this feel like your **LeetCode random test cases**. 😂

You don't choose the note.

The note is randomly called.

Your job:

> Hear the note name → locate it mentally → reach it.

### Basic version

Someone says:

> **C**

You immediately reach for C.

Then:

> **A**

Reach A.

Then:

> **F**

Reach F.

Then:

> **D**

Reach D.

### Important rule

**Don't stare at the keyboard.**

You can look briefly before starting, but once the exercise begins, try to keep your eyes away.

### Example set

```text
C
A
F
D
B
G
E
C
F
A
```

For every call:

```text
NOTE
 ↓
MENTAL LOCATION
 ↓
HAND MOVEMENT
 ↓
TOUCH
```

### Make it progressively harder

#### Level 1

Random white notes within one octave.

#### Level 2

Random white notes across multiple octaves.

Example:

> C → C → C → C

But each C is in a different octave.

#### Level 3

Random notes + random octaves.

Example:

> C4 → A3 → F4 → D5 → G3

Now you're training **true keyboard geography**.

### 🎯 Success condition

Start with:

> **15 random attempts**

Goal:

> **12+/15 correct**

Eventually:

> **20/20 correct**

# 1.7.6 — 👀 Blind Accuracy Check

This is the **feedback loop** that makes all the previous exercises work.

Never blindly repeat mistakes.

### The process

Every attempt follows:

```text
TARGET
   ↓
LOOK AWAY
   ↓
REACH
   ↓
TOUCH
   ↓
LOOK
   ↓
VERIFY
   ↓
CORRECT / WRONG
```

### Example

Target:

> **G**

You look away.

Reach.

Touch.

Look down.

Oops!

You touched F.

❌ Wrong.

Don't immediately move on.

Ask:

> “Why did I miss?”

Maybe you were:

* one key too far left
* one key too far right
* confused by the black-key landmark
* using the wrong octave
* moving too quickly

### Corrective repetition

If you miss G:

```text
G → Look → Touch
G → Look away → Reach
G → Blind reach
```

Do this **3 times correctly**.

Then continue.

# 🧠 Your Error Log

Buddy, I strongly recommend keeping a tiny mental or written error log.

For example:

```text
DATE: Aug 24

C → ✅
D → ✅
E → ❌ → touched D
F → ✅
G → ❌ → touched A
A → ✅
B → ✅

Weak notes:
E
G
```

Then give those weak notes extra repetitions.

This is exactly the same principle as debugging code:

> ❌ Find bug
> 🔍 Understand bug
> 🔧 Fix bug
> 🔁 Retest

# 🏆 Your Final Goal for Bucket 1.7

Don't think:

> “I need to memorize where every key is.”

Instead, train this:

> **Note → Mental Map → Hand → Key**

Eventually, when somebody says:

> **“F”**

your brain shouldn't need to consciously calculate:

> “Okay, two black keys... three black keys... where is F again...”

It should become:

> **F → ✋ → 🎹**

That automatic **brain-to-hand mapping** is exactly what we want before you move toward faster playing and more advanced keyboard skills. 🎹🔥
