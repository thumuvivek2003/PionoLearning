## 🎯 8.3.1 — Major Triad Recall

### What you are training

Instantly build any **major triad** from memory.

A major triad follows:

**Root + 4 semitones + 3 semitones**

Examples:

* C → C E G
* D → D F# A
* E → E G# B
* F → F A C
* G → G B D
* A → A C# E
* B → B D# F#

### Practice Session

**Stage 1 — Fixed chords**

Pick 5 chords and play them repeatedly.

Example:

`C → G → D → A → E`

Play each chord several times.

**Stage 2 — Random root**

Ask yourself randomly:

> "Give me a major chord."

Choose a root such as **Ab**.

Immediately think:

**Ab → C → Eb**

Then play it.

**Stage 3 — No-thinking drill**

Give yourself 10 random roots:

`F → Bb → E → C# → A → Db → G → Eb → B → Ab`

Your target is:

**See root → construct major triad → play.**

### 🎯 Goal

You should eventually construct a major triad in **under 2 seconds**.

---

# 🎯 8.3.2 — Minor Triad Recall

### What you are training

Instantly construct any minor triad.

Formula:

**Root + 3 semitones + 4 semitones**

Examples:

* C minor → C Eb G
* D minor → D F A
* E minor → E G B
* F minor → F Ab C
* G minor → G Bb D
* A minor → A C E
* B minor → B D F#

### Practice Session

**Stage 1 — Familiar minor chords**

Start with:

`Cm → Dm → Em → Fm → Gm → Am → Bm`

Play each slowly.

**Stage 2 — Random minor chords**

Randomly choose roots:

`Abm → C#m → Bbm → F#m → Ebm`

For each one:

1. Say the root.
2. Calculate the minor 3rd.
3. Find the perfect 5th.
4. Play all three notes.

**Stage 3 — Major vs Minor**

Now mix them:

`C major → D minor → F major → Bb minor → E major → Ab minor`

This is important because you are training your brain to recognize **chord quality**, not just chord roots.

### 🎯 Goal

See:

**"F#m"**

and immediately play:

**F#–A–C#**

without hesitation.

---

# 🎯 8.3.3 — Diminished Triads

### What you are training

Understand and instantly construct diminished triads.

Formula:

**Root + 3 semitones + 3 semitones**

Examples:

* Cdim → C Eb Gb
* Ddim → D F Ab
* Edim → E G Bb
* Fdim → F Ab B
* Gdim → G Bb Db
* Adim → A C Eb
* Bdim → B D F

Notice:

**Major:** 4 + 3
**Minor:** 3 + 4
**Diminished:** 3 + 3

### Practice Session

Take one root and compare all three:

**C major**

C – E – G

**C minor**

C – Eb – G

**C diminished**

C – Eb – Gb

Then do:

`D → E → F → G → A → B`

For each root, construct:

**Major → Minor → Diminished**

### 🎯 Goal

When you see **Gdim**, you should immediately know:

**G–Bb–Db**

without confusing it with G minor.

# 🎯 8.3.4 — Chord → Notes

### What you are training

This reverses the previous exercise.

Instead of:

> Notes → chord

you practice:

> **Chord name → notes**

### Practice Session

Write random chord names on paper.

For example:

```text
C
Am
F
Ddim
Bb
Em
C#m
G
Ab
Bdim
```

Pick one without looking ahead.

Example:

**C#m**

You must immediately say:

**C# – E – G#**

Then play it.

### Three levels

**Level 1 — Say the notes**

`F major → F A C`

**Level 2 — Find the notes**

Don't say them first. Find them directly.

**Level 3 — Play immediately**

See:

`Eb minor`

and immediately play:

**Eb–Gb–Bb**

### 🎯 Goal

Build the connection:

**Chord symbol → keyboard shape**

rather than:

**Chord symbol → long theoretical calculation → keyboard.**

# 🎯 8.3.5 — Notes → Chord

Now reverse the process.

You are given **three notes**, and you identify the chord.

For example:

**C–E–G**

Answer:

**C major**

Another:

**A–C–E**

Answer:

**A minor**

Another:

**B–D–F**

Answer:

**B diminished**

### Practice Session

Start with root-position chords.

Example:

```text
C E G
D F A
E G B
F A C
G B D
A C E
B D F
```

Identify each.

Then mix qualities:

```text
C Eb G
D F# A
E G Bb
F Ab C
G B D
A C# E
B D F
```

### Important next step

Don't always give yourself notes in root order.

For example:

**E–G–C**

You should still recognize:

**C major**

because the notes are the same.

Then:

**G–C–E**

also:

**C major**

### 🎯 Goal

Recognize the **chord identity**, regardless of note order.

# 🎯 8.3.6 — Random Chord Drill

🔥 This is the **core exercise** of the entire bucket.

You randomly generate a chord and immediately play it.

### Your random pool

**Major + Minor**

**Diminished**

Example sequence:


For every chord:

### Step 1

### Step 2

### Step 3

Identify the quality.

### Step 4

Know the three notes.

### Step 5

Play immediately.

### Step 6

Release and move to the next chord.

```text

```

Don't stop between chords.

Your objective is:

**Random → Think → Play → Release → Next**

### 🎯 Goal

Eventually you should be able to play **20 random chords continuously** with almost no hesitation.

# 🎯 8.3.7 — Chord Inversions

Now we introduce flexibility.

For a C major chord:

### Root position

**C–E–G**

### 1st inversion

**E–G–C**

### 2nd inversion

**G–C–E**

The chord remains **C major**.

Only the arrangement changes.

---

### Practice Session

Choose one chord.

Example:

**G major**

Practice:

```text
G–B–D
B–D–G
D–G–B
```

Then:

**A minor**

```text
A–C–E
C–E–A
E–A–C
```

Then:

**F major**

```text
F–A–C
A–C–F
C–F–A
```

### Random inversion drill

Now give yourself:

```text
C major — 2nd inversion
A minor — 1st inversion
F major — root
G major — 2nd inversion
D minor — 1st inversion
```

You must immediately play the requested shape.

### 🎯 Goal

Don't think:

> "Where should I move my fingers?"

Instead think:

> **"C major, second inversion."**

Your hand should automatically find:

**G–C–E**

# 🎯 8.3.8 — Chord Speed Drill

🔥 This is where everything comes together.

You now introduce a **timer**.

The objective isn't merely playing fast.

The objective is:

> **Reduce the time between seeing the chord and producing the correct sound.**

---

## 🟢 Stage 1 — 5-second drill

Give yourself 10 random chords.

You have up to **5 seconds per chord**.

Example:

```text
C
Am
F
D
Gm
Bb
Em
C#m
G
Bdim
```

---

## 🟡 Stage 2 — 3-second drill

Now reduce to:

**3 seconds per chord.**

The process should become:

**See → recognize → construct → play**

---

## 🟠 Stage 3 — 2-second drill

Now try:

**2 seconds per chord.**

Don't worry if you make occasional mistakes.

Accuracy still comes first.

---

## 🔴 Stage 4 — Continuous drill

Set a timer for **60 seconds**.

Generate as many random chords as possible.

For example:

```text
C → Am → F → G → Dm → Bb → Em → C → F#m → G
```

Count:

* Correct chords
* Incorrect chords
* Hesitations
* Chords requiring correction

Your score could be:

**8/10 correct**

Then next session:

**9/10**

Then:

**10/10**

# 🧠 The Most Important Progression

Don't try to make everything fast immediately.

Use this progression:

**Week 1**

> Accuracy → understand → play correctly

**Week 2**

> Recognition → reduce thinking

**Week 3**

> Randomization → remove predictability

**Week 4**

> Speed → continuous execution

Your final target should feel like this:

> **"F#m" → F# A C# → hand moves → chord sounds.**

No long calculation. No searching around the keyboard. No stopping.
