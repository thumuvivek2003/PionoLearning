## Bucket 5.6 — 🏠 Diatonic Chords

### 🎯 Overall Goal

By the end of this bucket, when someone says:

**Scale:**
C – D – E – F – G – A – B

**Chords:**
C – Dm – Em – F – G – Am – Bdim

# 🟢 Practice Session 1 — Chords from a Major Scale

### What are we learning?

We take **one major scale** and build a chord on **every note** of that scale.

Let's use **C Major**:

**C Major Scale**

C – D – E – F – G – A – B

Now we build a 3-note chord starting from each note, using **every other scale note**.

### On C

C → E → G

= **C Major**

### On D

D → F → A

= **D Minor**

### On E

E → G → B

= **E Minor**

### On F

F → A → C

= **F Major**

### On G

G → B → D

= **G Major**

### On A

A → C → E

= **A Minor**

### On B

B → D → F

= **B Diminished**

So:

**C Major scale → 7 natural chords**

| Scale Degree | Chord | Quality    |
| ------------ | ----- | ---------- |
| 1            | C     | Major      |
| 2            | Dm    | Minor      |
| 3            | Em    | Minor      |
| 4            | F     | Major      |
| 5            | G     | Major      |
| 6            | Am    | Minor      |
| 7            | Bdim  | Diminished |

### 🎹 Your practice

Play the scale:

**C D E F G A B C**

Then play:

**C → Dm → Em → F → G → Am → Bdim**

Don't rush.

Say aloud:

> C Major, D Minor, E Minor, F Major, G Major, A Minor, B Diminished.

### 🎯 Goal

You should understand:

> **The chords aren't random. They are created from the notes of the scale.**

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 2 — I Chord

The **I chord** is the chord built on scale degree 1.

In C Major:

**I = C**

Notes:

**C – E – G**

This is your **home chord**.

Think of it like your musical **home base**.

If you're playing:

**C → F → G → C**

that final C feels like:

> "Ahhh... we're home."

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**C → G → C**

Then:

**C → F → C**

Then:

**C → Am → F → C**

Listen carefully to how C feels like the place where everything settles.

### 🎯 Goal

When you hear/play a progression, start developing the instinct:

> **I = HOME**

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 3 — ii Chord

The **ii chord** is built from scale degree 2.

C Major:

C – D – E – F – G – A – B

Second note:

**D**

Build:

**D – F – A**

= **Dm**

Therefore:

**ii = Dm**

Notice the lowercase Roman numeral:

**ii**

Lowercase means **minor**.

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**C → Dm → C**

Then:

**C → Dm → G → C**

That second progression is extremely important:

**I → ii → V → I**

Listen to how Dm seems to move toward G, and G wants to resolve back to C.

### 🎯 Goal

Understand:

**ii = minor chord built on degree 2**

and begin hearing its **pull toward V**.

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 4 — iii Chord

The third scale note is:

**E**

Build using scale notes:

E – G – B

= **Em**

Therefore:

**iii = Em**

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**C → Em → C**

Then:

**C → Em → F → G → C**

### Important point

The iii chord is usually not as strong functionally as I, IV, or V.

At your level, don't worry about complicated harmonic theory.

Your first goal is simply:

> Recognize and play **iii = minor chord on degree 3**.

### 🎯 Goal

Instantly recognize:

**iii in C Major = Em**

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 5 — IV Chord

Fourth scale degree:

**F**

Build:

F – A – C

= **F Major**

Therefore:

**IV = F**

This is one of the most important chords in popular music.

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**C → F → C**

Then:

**C → F → G → C**

That's:

**I → IV → V → I**

This is one of the most important harmonic patterns to learn.

### Think of it like:

**I = Home**

**IV = Move away**

**V = Strong tension**

**I = Home**

### 🎯 Goal

Develop the sound of:

**I → IV → V → I**

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 6 — V Chord

Fifth scale degree:

**G**

Build:

G – B – D

= **G Major**

Therefore:

**V = G**

This chord is extremely important because it creates strong tension that wants to resolve to I.

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**G → C**

Then repeatedly:

**G → C**

Listen.

G feels like:

> "I need to go somewhere."

C feels like:

> "We're home."

Now play:

**C → F → G → C**

### 🎯 Goal

Develop the feeling:

**V → I = tension → resolution**

This is one of the most important sounds in tonal harmony.

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 7 — vi Chord

Sixth scale degree:

**A**

Build:

A – C – E

= **Am**

Therefore:

**vi = Am**

This chord is especially interesting because **Am is the relative minor chord of C Major**.

C Major:

C D E F G A B

Relative minor:

A B C D E F G

Same notes, different tonal center.

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**C → Am**

Then:

**C → G → Am → F**

This progression is extremely common.

### 🎯 Goal

Understand:

**vi = minor chord on degree 6**

and recognize:

**C Major ↔ A Minor**

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 8 — vii° Chord

This one is slightly different.

Seventh scale degree:

**B**

Build:

B – D – F

The intervals create a diminished triad.

Therefore:

**vii° = Bdim**

The little **°** means diminished.

### Why is B important?

Because B is the **leading tone** of C.

B wants to move upward to:

**C**

So:

**B → C**

has a very strong feeling of resolution.

And:

**Bdim → C**

has an even stronger harmonic pull.

### 🎹 Practice

Play:

**B → C**

Then:

**Bdim → C**

Listen carefully.

### 🎯 Goal

Understand:

> **vii° is built on the 7th scale degree and has strong movement toward I.**

Don't worry about mastering all the theory behind diminished harmony yet. Just make the sound familiar.

---

# 🟢 Practice Session 9 — Roman Numerals

Now we put everything together.

For **any major key**, the basic diatonic triad pattern is:

**I – ii – iii – IV – V – vi – vii°**

Notice the pattern:

**Major – Minor – Minor – Major – Major – Minor – Diminished**

You should memorize this pattern.

### C Major

| Roman Numeral | Chord |
| ------------- | ----- |
| I             | C     |
| ii            | Dm    |
| iii           | Em    |
| IV            | F     |
| V             | G     |
| vi            | Am    |
| vii°          | Bdim  |

### Why Roman numerals?

Because they allow you to think about the **function/position of a chord**, rather than just its letter name.

For example:

**C → F → G → C**

in C Major:

**I → IV → V → I**

But if we move to G Major:

**G → C → D → G**

the same harmonic structure is:

**I → IV → V → I**

That's the power of Roman numerals.

### 🎹 Your exercise

Play:

**I → IV → V → I**

in C Major.

Then try to identify the chords:

**C → F → G → C**

Say:

> I → IV → V → I

### 🎯 Goal

Start thinking:

> **"What degree is this chord?"**

instead of only:

> "What chord letter is this?"

# 🟢 Practice Session 10 — C Major Chord Family

Now we create your complete **C Major chord family**.

### 🎹 The family

**C Major**

C – E – G

**Dm**

D – F – A

**Em**

E – G – B

**F Major**

F – A – C

**G Major**

G – B – D

**Am**

A – C – E

**Bdim**

B – D – F

So memorize:

**C – Dm – Em – F – G – Am – Bdim**

And their Roman numerals:

**I – ii – iii – IV – V – vi – vii°**

# 🧠 The Most Important Pattern to Memorize

For **every Major scale**, memorize this:

**I = Major**
**ii = minor**
**iii = minor**
**IV = Major**
**V = Major**
**vi = minor**
**vii° = diminished**

So:

> **M – m – m – M – M – m – dim**

This pattern is the real heart of **Diatonic Chords**.

Once this becomes automatic, you can take **any major scale** and generate its entire chord family.
