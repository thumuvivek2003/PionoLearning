Absolutely buddy ❤️. This bucket is where you move from **“I know the thumb-under idea”** to actually making the thumb crossing feel natural.

The most important principle throughout all 6 sessions is:

> **The thumb does not jump awkwardly under the hand. The hand gently shifts/repositions so the thumb can continue the musical line.**

---

# 🖐️ LEVEL 2 — FINGER TECHNIQUE

## Bucket 2.7 — Finger Crossing

### 🎯 Main Goal

You want your brain and hand to learn:

**Play → prepare → cross → reposition → continue**

rather than:

**Play → stretch → get stuck → force the thumb**

We'll use **RH first**, because it's easier to understand visually. Once comfortable, mirror the same concepts with LH.

---

# 1️⃣ Practice — 1-2-3-1

### Basic RH crossing

This is your first true thumb-under exercise.

### Setup

Use the RH:

| Finger | Note |
| ------ | ---- |
| 1      | C    |
| 2      | D    |
| 3      | E    |
| 1      | F    |

So:

**C → D → E → F**

with:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 1**

### Step 1 — Extremely slowly

Play:

**C(1)**

then

**D(2)**

then

**E(3)**

Now instead of trying to stretch your hand to F:

👉 move the **thumb underneath/reposition the hand**

and play:

**F(1)**

So:

> **1 → 2 → 3 → thumb → 1 continues**

### What should happen?

After playing E with finger 3:

* Don't lift your whole hand dramatically.
* Don't twist your wrist.
* Don't force your thumb underneath.
* Let the thumb move toward the next position.
* The hand gently follows the thumb.

Think:

**3 → reposition → 1**

### Exercise

Do:

**C-D-E-F**

then stop.

Repeat:

**C-D-E-F**

10 times slowly.

Then reverse:

**F-E-D-C**

Don't worry about speed.

### 🎯 Learning goal

Your brain should start understanding:

> **“My thumb can take over when my current fingers run out of comfortable space.”**

---

# 2️⃣ Practice — 3-2-1-3

### Reverse movement

Now we teach your hand that crossing isn't only one-directional.

Use:

**E → D → C → F**

with:

**3 → 2 → 1 → 3**

The important part is:

**1 → 3**

Here, you need to reposition your hand so finger 3 can continue.

### Start slowly

Play:

**E(3)**

↓

**D(2)**

↓

**C(1)**

↓

reposition

↓

**F(3)**

Don't try to make this fast.

### Think of it as:

**3 → 2 → 1 → MOVE → 3**

The important learning isn't the musical pattern itself.

It's:

> **“After using my thumb, I can move my hand and continue with another finger.”**

### Practice sequence

Try:

**E-D-C-F**

Then:

**D-C-B-E**

Then:

**C-B-A-D**

Very slowly.

### 🎯 Learning goal

Develop the feeling:

> **“Crossing can happen in different directions and from different fingers.”**

---

# 3️⃣ Practice — 1-2-3-4-5-1

### Extended movement

Now we make the movement longer.

Start:

**C-D-E-F-G**

using:

**1-2-3-4-5**

Then you need to continue to another note.

For example:

**C-D-E-F-G-A**

The first five notes use:

**1-2-3-4-5**

Then you reposition and continue with:

**1**

### Important ⚠️

Don't think:

> “I have to somehow squeeze my thumb underneath finger 5.”

Instead think:

> **“My five fingers have reached the end of their comfortable position, so my hand moves to the next position.”**

### Practice

First:

**C-D-E-F-G**

Stop.

Then:

**C-D-E-F-G-A**

Stop.

Then:

**C-D-E-F-G-A-B**

Stop.

Eventually:

**C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C**

### Your job

Don't focus on speed.

Focus on whether the transition after **G** feels:

* smooth
* relaxed
* controlled
* quiet

### 🎯 Learning goal

Build the ability to continue a line **after your original five-finger position ends.**

---

# 4️⃣ Practice — Scale Fragments

### C-D-E-F-G-A

Now we apply crossing to actual musical movement.

Instead of treating crossing as an isolated exercise, we start making it feel like **normal playing**.

### Fragment 1

Play:

**C-D-E-F-G**

Use:

**1-2-3-4-5**

Then reposition.

### Fragment 2

Play:

**D-E-F-G-A**

Again:

**1-2-3-4-5**

Then reposition.

### Fragment 3

Play:

**E-F-G-A-B**

Again.

### Fragment 4

Now connect them:

**C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C**

The important moment is:

**C-D-E-F-G → reposition → A-B-C**

### Slow practice

Don't play the entire thing repeatedly at first.

Instead isolate the crossing:

**E-F-G | A**

Then:

**F-G-A | B**

Then:

**G-A-B | C**

You're teaching the hand:

> **“I know a position is ending, so I prepare my next position.”**

### 🎯 Learning goal

Crossing starts becoming a **musical movement**, rather than a special trick.

---

# 5️⃣ Practice — Crossing After Different Fingers

### Avoid one-pattern dependency

This one is VERY important for you.

If you only practice:

**1-2-3-1**

your brain may memorize:

> “Crossing = always after finger 3.”

That's not enough.

We want your hand to understand the **principle**.

---

## Pattern A — After finger 3

**1-2-3 → 1**

Example:

**C-D-E-F**

---

## Pattern B — After finger 4

**1-2-3-4 → 1**

Example:

**C-D-E-F-G**

Here you're learning to reposition after a longer sequence.

---

## Pattern C — Start from another position

Try:

**D-E-F-G-A**

Then:

**E-F-G-A-B**

Then:

**F-G-A-B-C**

The starting note keeps changing.

---

## Pattern D — Different starting finger

Don't always begin with finger 1.

For example, start:

**3-4-5 → reposition**

Then:

**2-3-4-5 → reposition**

The exact musical pattern is less important than teaching your hand:

> **“I don't need one memorized crossing pattern. I can reposition whenever the next notes require it.”**

### 🎯 Learning goal

Develop **adaptability**.

You don't want:

> “I know this one crossing exercise.”

You want:

> **“I understand when and how to move my hand.”**

---

# 6️⃣ Practice — Slow → Medium → Fast

### Build usable speed

This should be the **final stage**, not the starting point.

### 🐢 Stage 1 — Slow

Play the crossing extremely slowly.

Example:

**C-D-E-F**

Count:

**1... 2... 3... 1**

Make sure every note is clean.

Ask:

* Is my wrist relaxed?
* Is my thumb moving comfortably?
* Am I squeezing?
* Am I accidentally lifting my hand?
* Is the transition smooth?

---

### 🚶 Stage 2 — Medium

Once the movement feels automatic:

Increase the speed slightly.

Example:

**C-D-E-F-G-A**

Don't try to impress yourself.

Your goal is:

> **Same accuracy + slightly faster movement.**

---

### 🏃 Stage 3 — Fast

Only increase speed when:

**accuracy = stable**

**tension = low**

**movement = controlled**

If you start making mistakes:

👉 immediately slow down.

Don't train the mistake.

### 🎯 Golden rule

> **Speed is the RESULT of efficient movement, not something you force into the hand.**
