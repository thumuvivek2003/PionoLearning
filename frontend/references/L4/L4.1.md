# 🎼 4.1 — Major Scale Formula: Complete Practice Plan

## 🎯 4.1.1 — Whole Step

### What you need to understand

A **whole step (W)** means moving **2 semitones**.

On the keyboard:

**C → D**

There is one black key between them:

`C → C# → D`

So:

**C → D = 2 semitones = Whole Step**

Another example:

**E → F#**

`E → F → F#`

Again, 2 semitones.

### 🎹 Practice Session

**Step 1 — Find C**

Play C.

Then move:

* 1 key → C#
* 2 keys → D

Say aloud:

> **C to D = Whole Step**

Do this with different starting notes.

### Drill

Practice:

* C → D
* D → E
* F → G
* G → A
* A → B
* C# → D#
* D# → F
* F# → G#

Don't worry about scales yet.

### 🎯 Goal

You should eventually see:

**"2 semitones" → "Whole Step"**

instantly.

---

# 🎼 4.1.2 — Half Step

### What you need to understand

A **half step (H)** means moving to the **very next key**, whether black or white.

Examples:

**C → C#**

**E → F**

**B → C**

Notice something important:

There is **no black key between E and F**.

Therefore:

**E → F = Half Step**

And:

**B → C = Half Step**

### 🎹 Practice Session

Start from random keys and move **one key** to the right.

For example:

`C → C#`

`D → D#`

`E → F`

`F → F#`

`G → G#`

`A → A#`

`B → C`

Say:

> **One key = Half Step**

### 🎯 Goal

You should be able to look at two adjacent keys and immediately know:

**Half Step.**

---

# 🎼 4.1.3 — W-W-H-W-W-W-H

Now we reach the **heart of the major scale**.

The major scale formula is:

> **W – W – H – W – W – W – H**

Think of it as your **scale-building recipe**.

### 🎹 Example: C Major

Start at C.

Apply the formula:

**C**

⬇ W

**D**

⬇ W

**E**

⬇ H

**F**

⬇ W

**G**

⬇ W

**A**

⬇ W

**B**

⬇ H

**C**

So:

**C D E F G A B C**

### Practice Method

Don't immediately play the scale quickly.

Instead, say:

> C → W → D
> D → W → E
> E → H → F
> F → W → G
> G → W → A
> A → W → B
> B → H → C

### 🎯 Goal

Memorize:

**W-W-H-W-W-W-H**

without needing to look it up.

---

# 🎼 4.1.4 — Formula Starting From Any Note

This is where your real scale-building ability begins.

Don't memorize:

> C Major = C D E F G A B C

Instead learn:

> **Any starting note + W-W-H-W-W-W-H = Major Scale**

### 🎹 Example: G Major

Start:

**G**

Apply formula:

W → A
W → B
H → C
W → D
W → E
W → F#
H → G

Therefore:

**G A B C D E F# G**

Notice what happened?

You **constructed** the scale.

You didn't memorize it.

---

### 🎹 Example: D Major

Start:

**D**

W → E
W → F#
H → G
W → A
W → B
W → C#
H → D

Result:

**D E F# G A B C# D**

---

### 🎯 Practice Drill

Pick random starting notes.

For example:

**C → build C Major**

**D → build D Major**

**E → build E Major**

**F → build F Major**

**G → build G Major**

**A → build A Major**

**B → build B Major**

Then later:

**C# → build C# Major**

etc.

### 🎯 Goal

The question should change from:

> "What notes are in D Major?"

to:

> "Give me D. I can build the major scale."

That's a **much stronger skill**.

# 🎼 4.1.5 — Formula on Keyboard

Now connect the theory to **keyboard geography**.

This is extremely important for you because you're learning keyboard.

Don't just think:

> W = 2 semitones

Actually **see and feel the distance on the keyboard**.

### 🎹 Example: C Major

Play:

`C → D`

You physically moved **2 keys**.

Then:

`D → E`

Again **2 keys**.

Then:

`E → F`

Only **1 key**.

Then:

`F → G`

2 keys.

So visually:

**C — D — E | F — G — A — B | C**

The distances are:

**2 – 2 – 1 – 2 – 2 – 2 – 1**

That's another way of remembering:

> **W-W-H-W-W-W-H**

### 🔥 Important Conversion

| Musical Language | Keyboard Language |
| ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Whole Step       | 2 semitones       |
| Half Step        | 1 semitone        |
| W                | 2 keys            |
| H                | 1 key             |

So when you're sitting at the keyboard, think:

> **2 – 2 – 1 – 2 – 2 – 2 – 1**

### 🎯 Practice

Pick a starting key.

Don't play the scale yet.

Just physically move according to:

**2 → 2 → 1 → 2 → 2 → 2 → 1**

Then say the note names.

This develops your **keyboard geography + theory connection** simultaneously.
