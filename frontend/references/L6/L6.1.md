# 🎼 LEVEL 6 — MUSIC READING

## 📜 Bucket 6.1 — Staff & Musical Symbols

### 🎯 Your master routine

For almost every exercise in this bucket, use this sequence:

**LOOK → LOCATE → SAY → FIND → PLAY → CHECK**

For example:

**Look:** 👀 See a note on the staff
↓
**Locate:** Is it on a line or space? Which one?
↓
**Say:** "This is G"
↓
**Find:** Locate G on your keyboard
↓
**Play:** Press G
↓
**Check:** Was I correct?

Initially, you can take your time. Later, we'll increase the speed.

# 1️⃣ Staff — Learn the 5 horizontal lines

### 🎯 Objective

Understand that the **staff is the basic map on which musical notes are written.**

A staff has:

* 5 lines
* 4 spaces

The lines and spaces are counted **from bottom to top**.

```text
Line 5  ─────────────

Space 4

Line 4  ─────────────

Space 3

Line 3  ─────────────

Space 2

Line 2  ─────────────

Space 1

Line 1  ─────────────
```

### 🧠 Practice

Don't worry about note names yet.

Look at a blank staff and point to:

```text
Line 1
Line 2
Line 3
Line 4
Line 5
```

Then point to:

```text
Space 1
Space 2
Space 3
Space 4
```

### 🎹 Keyboard connection

At this stage, **don't play anything**.

You're training your eyes to understand the vertical geography of the staff.

### ⏱️ Practice

**3–5 minutes**

### ✅ Mastery test

I randomly point to a line and ask:

> "Which line?"

You should immediately answer:

> "Line 4."

No counting from the bottom every time.

---

# 2️⃣ Lines — Identify line positions 1–5

Now we're going one step deeper.

You already know that there are five lines.

Your job is to recognize their positions quickly.

### 🧠 Exercise

Take random notes that are sitting **directly on lines**.

For example:

```text
● ───────  ← Line 5

─────── ●  ← Line 4

● ───────  ← Line 3

─────── ●  ← Line 2

● ───────  ← Line 1
```

Don't identify the note yet.

Just say:

> "Line 5."

> "Line 2."

> "Line 4."

### 🎯 Important

Train yourself to recognize:

**vertical position → line number**

rather than:

**counting lines every time.**

### 🎹 Later connection

Once you're comfortable, we'll attach note names to these positions.

For example, in treble clef:

```text
Line 1 = E
Line 2 = G
Line 3 = B
Line 4 = D
Line 5 = F
```

But **don't try to memorize everything at once**.

### ⏱️ Practice

**5 minutes**

### ✅ Mastery

Show yourself 20 random line notes.

Try to identify the line number with almost no hesitation.

# 3️⃣ Spaces — Identify spaces 1–4

Now we do exactly the same thing with spaces.

Remember:

```text
Line 5 ─────────

       Space 4

Line 4 ─────────

       Space 3

Line 3 ─────────

       Space 2

Line 2 ─────────

       Space 1

Line 1 ─────────
```

### 🧠 Exercise

Look at random notes positioned in spaces.

Don't ask:

> "What note is this?"

Ask:

> **"Which space?"**

For example:

```text
───────
   ●        ← Space 4
───────

───────
   ●        ← Space 3
───────
```

### 🎯 Goal

Your eyes should eventually recognize:

**Space 1 / Space 2 / Space 3 / Space 4**

almost instantly.

### ⏱️ Practice

**5 minutes**

### ✅ Mastery

20 random space notes.

Aim for:

**18–20 correct without hesitation.**

# 4️⃣ Notes — Understand noteheads and their position

Now we introduce the actual **note**.

The important concept is:

> **The vertical position of a note tells us its pitch.**

For example:

```text
        ●

        higher pitch
           ↑


        ●


        ●

           ↓
        lower pitch
```

The notehead is the part that sits on the staff.

### 🧠 Your exercise

Look at two notes.

Ask:

> Which one is higher?

Then:

> Which one is lower?

Don't worry about the exact note names initially.

### 🎹 Keyboard connection

Now connect this to your keyboard:

```text
Staff note moves UP
        ↓
Pitch goes HIGHER
        ↓
Move toward higher keys

Staff note moves DOWN
        ↓
Pitch goes LOWER
        ↓
Move toward lower keys
```

This is a **very important concept**.

You're beginning to connect:

**paper geography → keyboard geography**

### ⏱️ Practice

**5 minutes**

### ✅ Mastery

Given two notes, immediately tell:

> "Second note is higher."

or

> "Second note is lower."

# 5️⃣ Note Direction — Higher / Lower

This is one of the most useful skills for sight-reading.

You don't always need to identify every note individually.

Sometimes you can recognize **movement**.

For example:

```text
●
    ●
        ●
            ●
```

The melody is moving:

**UP → UP → UP**

Therefore:

**pitch → rising**

---

Another example:

```text
            ●
        ●
    ●
●
```

That's:

**DOWN → DOWN → DOWN**

### 🎹 Keyboard

Your hand should begin to understand:

```text
Staff goes ↑
Keyboard pitch goes ↑

Staff goes ↓
Keyboard pitch goes ↓
```

### 🧠 Exercise

Look at 3–5 notes and say only:

```text
UP
DOWN
SAME
UP
DOWN
```

Don't name the notes.

This trains your **visual pattern recognition**.

### ⏱️ Practice

**5–7 minutes**

### ✅ Mastery

You should be able to look at a short sequence and immediately recognize its contour.

# 6️⃣ Clefs — Understand what a clef does

Now we introduce something extremely important.

A staff by itself doesn't tell you exactly which pitches the lines and spaces represent.

The **clef gives the staff its pitch reference.**

Think of the clef as a **decoder**.

### 🎯 Simple analogy

Imagine the staff is a map:

```text
5 lines
4 spaces
```

The clef tells you:

> "This is how you should interpret this map."

That's why:

**Treble clef ≠ Bass clef**

The same vertical position can represent a different note depending on the clef.

### 🎹 Why this matters

Since you're learning keyboard, eventually you'll encounter:

```text
Treble staff → mostly right-hand territory

Bass staff → mostly left-hand territory
```

Although real keyboard music can cross these boundaries.

### ⏱️ Practice

**5 minutes**

Your first goal is simply:

> Recognize the clef before reading the notes.

### 🎯 Your core treble-clef map

The lines are:

```text
F
D
B
G
E
```

Bottom → top:

```text
Line 5 = F
Line 4 = D
Line 3 = B
Line 2 = G
Line 1 = E
```

The spaces are:

```text
E
C
A
F
```

Bottom → top:

```text
Space 4 = F
Space 3 = A
Space 2 = C
Space 1 = E
```

So:

**Lines → E G B D F**

**Spaces → F A C E**

You may know the mnemonic:

**See note → know note**
