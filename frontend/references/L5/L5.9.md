# LEVEL 5 — 🎶 CHORDS & HARMONY

## Bucket 5.9 — 👂 Chord Ear Training

### 🎯 Main Goal

By the end of this bucket, you should be able to hear a chord and quickly answer things like:

* Is it **major or minor?**
* What is the **root?**
* What is the **bass note?**
* Is it **root position or an inversion?**
* Is it functioning like **I, IV, or V?**
* Is the progression moving in a familiar way?
* Can I **sing the root**?
* Can I hear → identify → immediately play it?

---

# 🥇 5.9.1 — Major vs Minor

### 🎯 Goal

Train your ear to immediately recognize the emotional/intervallic difference between **major and minor chords**.

### 🎹 What to practice

Start with:

* C major = C–E–G
* C minor = C–Eb–G

Then gradually use other roots:

* D major / D minor
* E major / E minor
* F major / F minor
* G major / G minor
* A major / A minor

### 🧠 Practice Method

**Round 1 — Comparison**

Play:

> C major → C minor → C major → C minor

Don't look at your hands.

Ask yourself:

> "Which one sounds major?"

Then reverse the order randomly.

### Round 2 — Blind identification

Play one chord without looking.

Example:

> 🎹 C major

Ask:

> "Major or minor?"

Answer **before touching the keyboard again**.

Then check.

### Round 3 — Random roots

Have someone/randomly choose:

> Dm → G → Am → F → C → Em

Identify only:

> Major / Minor

### 🎯 Target

You should eventually identify the quality within **1–2 seconds**.

### ⚠️ Important

Don't rely only on the "happy/sad" feeling.

Listen specifically to the **third**:

* Major third → major
* Minor third → minor

This becomes extremely important later.

# 🥈 5.9.2 — Root Note Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Hear a chord and determine:

> **"What note is the chord built from?"**

For example:

🎹 C–E–G

You hear it and think:

> **C**

### 🧠 Practice Method

Start with only 3 chords:

> C major
> F major
> G major

Play them randomly.

Example:

> G → C → F → G → F → C

Your task:

**Hear → say the root → play the root.**

### 🎹 Keyboard connection

After hearing G major:

1. Say **G**
2. Find G on the keyboard
3. Play G
4. Check against the chord

### Round 2

Expand to:

> C, D, E, F, G

Then:

> C, D, E, F, G, A, B

### Round 3

Mix major and minor:

> C
> Am
> F
> Dm
> G
> Em

You only identify the **root**, not quality.

### 🎯 Target

Hear:

> 🎹 Am

and immediately think:

> **A**

This creates the connection:

**Sound → Note name → Keyboard location**

# 🥉 5.9.3 — Chord Bass Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Learn the difference between:

> **Root = what chord it is**

and

> **Bass = lowest note currently being played**

This is extremely important for inversions.

### Example

C major:

**C–E–G**

Root = C
Bass = C

But:

**E–G–C**

Root = C
Bass = E

And:

**G–C–E**

Root = C
Bass = G

### 🧠 Practice Method

Play:

> C–E–G

Ask:

**"What is the lowest note?"**

Answer:

> C

Then play:

> E–G–C

Ask:

> "What is the lowest note?"

Answer:

> E

Then:

> G–C–E

Answer:

> G

### 🔥 Important Drill

Play the three versions randomly:

```text
C–E–G
E–G–C
G–C–E
```

Your first job:

**Identify the bass.**

Don't worry about the chord name yet.

### 🎯 Target

Hear the lowest note almost immediately.

This prepares you for:

**5.9.4 Root Position vs Inversion**

# 🏆 5.9.4 — Root Position vs Inversion

### 🎯 Goal

Hear that the **same chord** can sound different depending on which note is in the bass.

Use C major:

```text
Root position:
C–E–G

1st inversion:
E–G–C

2nd inversion:
G–C–E
```

### 🧠 Practice Method

First play all three slowly.

Listen carefully.

Then close your eyes.

Play one randomly.

Ask yourself:

> "Which version did I hear?"

Answer:

* Root position
* 1st inversion
* 2nd inversion

### Round 2

Don't just identify the position.

Identify:

> **Chord + bass**

Example:

You hear:

> E–G–C

Think:

> **C major, E bass**

### Round 3

Use other chords:

```text
F–A–C
A–C–F
C–F–A
```

Then:

```text
G–B–D
B–D–G
D–G–B
```

### 🎯 Target

Eventually:

> Hear E–G–C
> Think **C major / E bass / 1st inversion**

This is a **very valuable keyboard skill**.

# 🎵 5.9.5 — I vs IV vs V

### 🎯 Goal

Train your ear to recognize the three most important harmonic functions.

In C major:

| Function | Chord |
| -------- | ----- |
| I        | C     |
| IV       | F     |
| V        | G     |

### 🧠 Practice Method

First establish the key.

Play:

> C → F → G → C

Then isolate them.

Play:

> C

Listen.

Play:

> F

Listen.

Play:

> G

Listen.

### Round 2 — Random

Play:

> F → C → G → F → G → C

Without looking, identify:

> I / IV / V

### Round 3 — Functional feeling

Train yourself to hear:

**I**

→ feels like **home**

**IV**

→ feels like moving away

**V**

→ creates strong **tension/pull toward I**

So:

> G → C

should start sounding like:

> **V → I**

### 🎯 Target

Hear a chord in C major and think:

> **I / IV / V**

rather than merely:

> C / F / G

That's the beginning of **functional ear training**.

# 🎶 5.9.6 — Major Progression Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Hear common chord movements and recognize/predict what might come next.

Start with:

### Progression 1

```text
I – IV – V – I

C – F – G – C
```

### Progression 2

```text
I – V – VI – IV

C – G – Am – F
```

### Progression 3

```text
I – VI – IV – V

C – Am – F – G
```

### 🧠 Practice Method

First play the progression while watching.

Then:

**Look away from the keyboard.**

Listen.

Try to identify each chord.

Example:

> 🎧 C → G → Am → F

You think:

> I → V → vi → IV

### 🔥 Prediction Drill

Play:

> C → F → G → ...

Stop.

Ask:

> **"What do I expect next?"**

Your answer should eventually become:

> **C / I**

Then check by playing it.

This develops **harmonic prediction**, which is very useful when playing songs.

### 🎯 Target

Don't just recognize chords individually.

Start hearing **the movement between chords**.

# 🌙 5.9.7 — Minor Progression Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Develop the same ability inside minor keys.

Start with A minor.

Basic chords:

```text
i = Am
iv = Dm
V = E
```

Practice:

```text
Am → Dm → E → Am
```

Also:

```text
Am → F → C → G
```

### 🧠 Practice Method

First establish:

> **Am = home**

Then play:

> Am → Dm → E → Am

Listen to the different roles.

### Round 2

Randomly play:

> Am
> Dm
> E
> F
> C
> G

Identify the chord.

### Round 3

Play a progression and identify the movement.

Example:

> Am → F → C → G

Try to hear:

> i → VI → III → VII

### 🎯 Target

You want minor harmony to stop sounding like "random minor chords."

You should begin hearing:

> **home → movement → tension → resolution**

### 🎯 Goal

Identify the **type of chord** purely by sound.

Start with:

### Level 2


```text

```

### Level 3

Use different roots:

```text
C
Cm
C7

F

Gm

```

### 🧠 Practice Method

Play one chord.

Don't look.

Ask:

> "What quality is this?"

Answer:

> Major / Minor / 7th

### 🔥 Important

Don't try to memorize individual sounds like:

> "C7 sounds like this."

Instead learn the **character of the chord quality**.

Eventually you should hear:

> 🎧 "That's a dominant 7th."

even when the root changes.

### 🎯 Target

Identify the quality in **1–2 seconds**.

# 🎤 5.9.9 — Sing the Root

### 🎯 Goal

This is one of the best exercises for connecting your **ear and inner pitch**.

Play:

> C major

Then try to sing:

> **C**

You don't need to sing loudly.

Even humming is fine.

### 🧠 Step-by-step

1. Play C major.
2. Let it ring.
3. Stop playing.
4. Sing/hum C.
5. Play C again.
6. Compare.

### Round 2

Do the same with:

> F major

Sing:

> F

Then:

> G major → sing G

### Round 3 — Harder

Play the chord.

Stop playing.

Wait **3–5 seconds**.

Then sing the root.

Only afterward check it.

### 🔥 Advanced version

Play:

> C major

Then sing:

> C

while the chord continues.

Then play the root separately.

You are training your brain to extract one specific pitch from a group of notes.

### 🎯 Target

Eventually you hear:

> 🎧 C major

and internally feel:

> **C**

without needing the keyboard immediately.
