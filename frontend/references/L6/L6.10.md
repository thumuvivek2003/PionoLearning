## 1️⃣ Note Recognition Speed

### 🎯 Goal

Identify a written note and immediately know the corresponding keyboard key.

### What you practice

Start with individual notes rather than melodies.

For example:

**Treble Clef:**

* C4
* D4
* E4
* F4
* G4
* A4
* B4
* C5
* D5

Look at the note → identify it → locate the key.

### Practice method

**Round 1 — Slow**

* Look at one note.
* Say the note name.
* Find the key.
* Play it.

**Round 2 — Faster**

* Look at the note.
* Don't say the name aloud.
* Immediately play the key.

**Round 3 — Random**
Use randomly mixed notes.

Example:

`E → C → G → D → A → F → B`

### Important rule

Don't count lines and spaces every time.

Eventually you should see:

> Note → Key

rather than:

> Note → count lines → calculate note → find key

### Daily target

**5–10 minutes**

### Success criterion

You should be able to identify common notes almost automatically.

---

# 2️⃣ Pattern Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Stop reading music as individual notes.

Instead, start seeing **groups and shapes**.

For example:

`C D E F G`

Instead of thinking:

> C... D... E... F... G...

you should recognize:

> **Ascending 5-note pattern**

### Practice patterns

#### Pattern A — Ascending

`C D E F G`

#### Pattern B — Descending

`G F E D C`

#### Pattern C — Repeated

`C C D D E E`

#### Pattern D — Wave

`C D E D C D E`

#### Pattern E — Broken pattern

`C E G E C`

### What to look for

When looking at a musical passage, ask:

* Is it going upward?
* Is it going downward?
* Is it repeating?
* Is the same shape appearing again?
* Is it stepwise?
* Is it skipping?

### Daily exercise

Take a short 1–2 measure passage.

**Don't play it immediately.**

First visually identify its pattern.

Then play.

### Success criterion

You should begin seeing:

> **groups → shapes → musical ideas**

instead of individual notes.

---

# 3️⃣ Interval Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Recognize the **distance between notes** visually.

This is extremely important for sight reading.

Instead of:

> C → E = C...D...E

you want:

> C → E = **3rd**

### Start with

**Step / 2nd**

C → D

**3rd**

C → E

**4th**

C → F

**5th**

C → G

Then gradually:

6th, 7th, octave.

### Practice method

Look at two consecutive notes.

Before playing, identify:

**Same?**

**Step?**

**Skip?**

**Large jump?**

Then play them.

### Example

Imagine:

`C → D → E → G → C`

Think:

> step → step → 3rd → 5th

rather than naming every note individually.

### Daily target

**5 minutes**

### Success criterion

You should visually recognize small intervals without calculating every note.

---

# 4️⃣ Direction Recognition

### 🎯 Goal

Immediately recognize whether the melody is moving:

⬆️ Up
⬇️ Down
➡️ Same

This is one of the simplest but most powerful sight-reading skills.

### Practice

Take a sequence such as:

`C D E G F E D`

Before playing, identify:

`↑ ↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ↓`

Then play.

Another:

`G E F D E C`

Direction:

`↓ ↑ ↓ ↑ ↓`

### Advanced version

Don't identify exact notes initially.

Look only at movement.

For example:

> ↑ ↑ ↓ → ↑ ↓ ↓

Then play the passage.

### Why this matters

Your eyes begin following the **movement of the music**, instead of stopping at every note.

### Daily target

**3–5 minutes**

### Success criterion

Your eyes should naturally notice melodic direction.

---

# 5️⃣ Rhythm + Notes

### 🎯 Goal

Now combine:

**Pitch + Rhythm**

This is where actual music reading starts becoming musical.

You need to recognize:

* Note
* Duration
* Rest
* Beat
* Rhythm pattern

### Practice sequence

Don't immediately play.

First:

### Step 1 — Clap rhythm

For example:

`♩ ♪ ♪ | ♩ ♩`

Clap it.

### Step 2 — Say note names

For example:

`C D E F`

without worrying about playing.

### Step 3 — Combine

Read:

> C + quarter note
> D + eighth note
> E + eighth note
> F + quarter note

### Step 4 — Play

Now play the passage with the correct rhythm.

### Important rule

**Never sacrifice rhythm just to find notes.**

If necessary, slow down.

### Daily target

**10 minutes**

### Success criterion

You can read simple pitch + rhythm combinations without stopping.

---

# 6️⃣ Key Signature + Notes

### 🎯 Goal

Read notes correctly while understanding the **key signature**.

This prevents you from treating every accidental as a surprise.

For example, if you are in:

**G Major**

you already know:

> F → F♯

So when you see F, your brain should understand the context.

### Practice order

Start with:

**C Major**

No sharps/flats.

Then:

**G Major**

1 sharp.

Then:

**F Major**

1 flat.

Then gradually:

D Major → B♭ Major → A Major → E♭ Major...

### Practice method

Before playing a passage:

### Step 1

Look at the key signature.

### Step 2

Say the scale/key mentally.

### Step 3

Identify affected notes.

### Step 4

Read the passage.

### Example

If the key is G Major:

Don't think:

> F → Oh, there's an accidental.

Think:

> F is normally sharp in this key.

### Daily target

**5 minutes**

### Success criterion

Key signatures should become a **background awareness**, not something that interrupts your reading.

# 7️⃣ Slow Sight Reading

### 🎯 Goal

Build the foundation for reading an **unfamiliar piece**.

This is extremely important.

Take a piece you have **never practiced before**.

### Before playing

Spend **20–30 seconds** looking at it.

Check:

* Clef
* Key signature
* Time signature
* Starting note
* Starting position
* Rhythm
* Repeated patterns
* Difficult sections
* Large jumps

Then start.

### Most important rule

🚫 **DO NOT STOP**

If you make a mistake:

Keep going.

This is different from normal practice.

You are training your brain to maintain the musical flow.

### Example

You miss one note.

Don't:

> STOP → find note → restart

Instead:

> mistake → recover → continue

### Daily target

**10 minutes**

### Success criterion

You can complete a simple unfamiliar piece without stopping.

# 8️⃣ Medium Sight Reading

### 🎯 Goal

Gradually increase difficulty while maintaining continuity.

Once easy pieces become comfortable, introduce:

* Larger intervals
* More rhythm variation
* More notes
* Faster passages
* More key signatures
* Longer phrases
* More hand movement

### Difficulty progression

#### Level A

5-finger patterns

↓

#### Level B

Simple scales

↓

#### Level C

Intervals

↓

#### Level D

Simple melodies

↓

#### Level E

Two-hand simple pieces

↓

#### Level F

Moderately difficult unfamiliar pieces

### Practice rule

Don't increase difficulty too quickly.

You want:

> **80–90% accuracy + continuous playing**

rather than:

> 50% accuracy + lots of stopping.

### Daily target

**10–15 minutes**

# 9️⃣ Timed Reading

### 🎯 Goal

Train your brain to read under a time constraint.

This becomes particularly useful for your contest.

### Exercise

Choose an unfamiliar piece.

Set a timer:

**1 minute**

Read continuously.

Then:

**2 minutes**

Then:

**3 minutes**

### Example challenge

Give yourself:

> **30 seconds preparation**

Then:

> **2 minutes performance**

During those 2 minutes:

🚫 No stopping
🚫 No restarting
🚫 No correcting previous notes

Just continue.

### Track three things

After each attempt record:

**Accuracy:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Continuity:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rhythm:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Example

| Attempt | Accuracy | Continuity | Rhythm |
| ------- | -------: | ---------: | -----: |
| 1       |      3/5 |        2/5 |    3/5 |
| 2       |      4/5 |        4/5 |    3/5 |
| 3       |      4/5 |        5/5 |    4/5 |

Your objective is not simply to play faster.

It's:

> **Read accurately while maintaining flow under time pressure.**

# 🔟 Contest Simulation

### 🎯 Goal

This is the **final application** of everything in Bucket 6.10.

You simulate exactly what your contest reading situation might feel like.

### Setup

Choose a completely unfamiliar piece.

Don't practice it beforehand.

Then follow:

### Phase 1 — Preparation

Give yourself:

**30–60 seconds**

Look at:

* Clef
* Key signature
* Time signature
* Starting position
* Rhythm
* Patterns
* Difficult jumps
* Repeated sections

### Phase 2 — Mental preview

Quickly imagine:

> Where does the melody go?

> Where are the difficult rhythms?

> Where are the large jumps?

### Phase 3 — Start

Begin playing.

### Phase 4 — No stopping

If you make an error:

**KEEP GOING.**

This is one of the most important contest skills.

### Phase 5 — Finish

Don't restart because of mistakes.

Finish the piece.

### Phase 6 — Evaluation

Immediately score yourself:

| Skill               | Score |
| ------------------- | ----: |
| Note accuracy       |   /10 |
| Rhythm              |   /10 |
| Continuity          |   /10 |
| Key signature       |   /10 |
| Pattern recognition |   /10 |
| Confidence          |   /10 |

Then identify your **single biggest weakness**.

Work on that weakness in your next session.

# ⭐ Most Important Rule for Reading Fluency

Buddy, remember this one principle:

> **Sight reading practice is NOT the same as learning a piece.**

When learning a piece, you can:

**Stop → Correct → Repeat → Memorize**

But during sight reading, you must train:

**See → Understand → Play → Continue**

Even when you make a mistake.

That **continuous visual-to-keyboard connection** is exactly what will make your reading speed improve. 🎹🔥

And for your contest, I would give **Bucket 6.10 a high priority**, especially **Note Recognition + Pattern Recognition + Interval Recognition + Sight Reading + Timed Reading**, because together they directly build the ability to look at unfamiliar music and keep playing.
