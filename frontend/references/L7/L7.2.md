# 🎼 LEVEL 7 — 🎹 PLAYING PATTERNS

## 🎶 Bucket 7.2 — Broken Chords

### 🎯 Overall Goal

By the end of Bucket 7.2, you should be able to:

* See a chord such as **C major = C–E–G**
* Know its three notes immediately
* Play them **one after another** rather than simultaneously
* Move both **upward and downward**
* Play broken chords smoothly without stopping
* Change between C, F, G and eventually other major/minor chords
* Maintain a steady rhythm while playing

---

## 🎵 7.2.1 — 1–3–5 Pattern

### What you are learning

For a major triad, the numbers **1–3–5** represent:

**1st note → 3rd note → 5th note**

For C major:

**C → E → G**

Instead of pressing C-E-G together, you play:

**C → E → G**

### 🎹 Practice

Start with your right hand.

Use:

* C = Finger 1
* E = Finger 3
* G = Finger 5

Play:

**C–E–G**

Then return to C:

**C–E–G–C**

### Practice routine

**Round 1:** C–E–G slowly × 10
**Round 2:** C–E–G–C × 10
**Round 3:** C–E–G–E × 10

Start around **♩ = 50–60 BPM**.

### 🎯 Focus

Don't try to play fast.

Your goal is:

**equal timing + relaxed fingers + correct notes**

Count:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 4**

with one note on each count.

---

# 🎵 7.2.2 — 5–3–1 Pattern

Now reverse the movement.

Instead of:

**1–3–5**

you play:

**5–3–1**

For C major:

**G → E → C**

### 🎹 Fingers

Right hand:

* G = 5
* E = 3
* C = 1

Practice:

**G–E–C**

Then:

**G–E–C–E**

### Practice routine

* G–E–C × 10
* G–E–C–E × 10
* C–E–G–E–C × 10

### 🎯 Focus

Pay attention to the **direction change**.

You should feel:

**ascending → descending**

and eventually be able to reverse direction without hesitation.

---

# 🎵 7.2.3 — C Major Broken Chord

Now we turn the basic pattern into a musical pattern.

### Chord

**C major = C–E–G**

### Pattern

**C–E–G–E**

This is extremely important because this type of pattern appears frequently in keyboard playing.

### 🎹 Fingers

**1–3–5–3**

So:

**C(1) → E(3) → G(5) → E(3)**

### Practice levels

**Level A — Slow**

C–E–G–E
× 10 repetitions

**Level B — Continuous**

C–E–G–E | C–E–G–E
× 10

**Level C — Rhythm**

Play four notes evenly:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 4**

### 🎯 Target

You should eventually be able to play:

**C–E–G–E | C–E–G–E | C–E–G–E**

without stopping between repetitions.

---

# 🎵 7.2.4 — F Major Broken Chord

Now move the exact same concept to F major.

### Chord

**F major = F–A–C**

### Pattern

**F–A–C–A**

### 🎹 Fingers

Right hand:

**1–3–5–3**

Play:

**F(1) → A(3) → C(5) → A(3)**

### Practice

First:

**F–A–C–A × 10**

Then:

**F–A–C–A | F–A–C–A × 10**

Then alternate:

**C–E–G–E | F–A–C–A**

### 🎯 Why this is important

You're training your brain to recognize that the **same physical pattern** can be moved to another chord.

The pattern remains:

**1–3–5–3**

Only the starting note changes.

---

# 🎵 7.2.5 — G Major Broken Chord

### Chord

**G major = G–B–D**

### Pattern

**G–B–D–B**

### 🎹 Fingers

**1–3–5–3**

Practice:

**G–B–D–B × 10**

Then continuously:

**G–B–D–B | G–B–D–B**

### 🔥 Important exercise

Now combine all three:

**C–E–G–E**

↓

**F–A–C–A**

↓

**G–B–D–B**

Play each four times.

Then:

**C → F → G → C**

This begins developing your ability to **connect chords**, which is much more useful musically than practicing each chord in isolation.

---

# 🎵 7.2.6 — Major Chord Broken Patterns

Now expand beyond only C, F and G.

Start with these major chords:

| Chord | Notes  | Pattern   |
| ----- | ------ | --------- |
| C     | C–E–G  | C–E–G–E   |
| F     | F–A–C  | F–A–C–A   |
| G     | G–B–D  | G–B–D–B   |
| D     | D–F#–A | D–F#–A–F# |
| A     | A–C#–E | A–C#–E–C# |
| E     | E–G#–B | E–G#–B–G# |

### 🎹 Daily drill

Choose **3 chords per day**.

For each chord:

**1–3–5–3 × 10**

Then connect them:

**Chord 1 → Chord 2 → Chord 3**

### 🎯 Goal

Eventually, when someone says:

> **"D major broken chord"**

you should immediately think:

**D–F#–A**

without searching for the notes.

---

# 🎵 7.2.7 — Minor Chord Broken Patterns

Now introduce minor chords.

The important difference is the **minor 3rd**.

For example:

### C major

**C–E–G**

### C minor

**C–E♭–G**

So the pattern becomes:

**C–E♭–G–E♭**

### 🎹 Start with these

| Chord | Notes  | Broken Pattern |
| ----- | ------ | -------------- |
| Am    | A–C–E  | A–C–E–C        |
| Dm    | D–F–A  | D–F–A–F        |
| Em    | E–G–B  | E–G–B–G        |
| Cm    | C–E♭–G | C–E♭–G–E♭      |

### Practice

For each chord:

**1–3–5–3 × 10**

Then compare:

**C major → C minor**

**C–E–G–E**

versus

**C–E♭–G–E♭**

### 🎯 Important learning outcome

You want your ear and fingers to recognize:

**Major = brighter sound**

**Minor = darker/sadder sound**

Don't just memorize the finger movement—**listen to the difference**.

---

# 🎵 7.2.8 — Continuous Broken Chords

🔥 This is the final and most important practice.

Now we remove the pauses.

Instead of:

**C–E–G–E**

*pause*

**F–A–C–A**

*pause*

you want:

**C–E–G–E | F–A–C–A | G–B–D–B | C–E–G–E**

### 🎹 Exercise 1 — One chord

C–E–G–E continuously for **30 seconds**.

Don't stop.

---

### 🎹 Exercise 2 — Three chords

**C–E–G–E | F–A–C–A | G–B–D–B**

Repeat for **1 minute**.

---

### 🎹 Exercise 3 — Chord progression

Practice:

**C → F → G → C**

Using:

**C–E–G–E | F–A–C–A | G–B–D–B | C–E–G–E**

---

### 🎹 Exercise 4 — Reverse direction

Practice:

**C–E–G–E**

then:

**G–E–C–E**

Then connect:

**C–E–G–E | G–E–C–E**

### 🎯 Tempo progression

Don't rush the BPM.

**Stage 1:** 50 BPM — accuracy
**Stage 2:** 60 BPM — consistency
**Stage 3:** 70 BPM — smoothness
**Stage 4:** 80 BPM — fluency
**Stage 5:** 90+ BPM — performance

Only increase the tempo when you can play the current tempo **without mistakes or tension**.
