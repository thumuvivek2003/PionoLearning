## 🎹 1. Simple 2-Chord Songs

### 🎯 Goal

Become comfortable changing between two chords without stopping.

Start with very easy progressions such as:

* C → G
* C → F
* Am → F
* G → C
* Am → G

### Practice Session

**Step 1 — Chord familiarity**

* Play C for 4 beats.
* Play G for 4 beats.
* Repeat 10 times.

**Step 2 — Slow changes**
Use a metronome around **60 BPM**.

Count:

> 1 - 2 - 3 - 4 → C
> 1 - 2 - 3 - 4 → G

Don't stop between chords.

**Step 3 — Different rhythms**

Try:

> C — — — | G — — —

Then:

> C ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ | G ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓

Then:

> C ↓ — ↓ — | G ↓ — ↓ —

### 🎵 Mini Challenge

Play the progression continuously for **2 minutes** without stopping.

### ✅ You should be able to:

* Change chords automatically.
* Keep the beat while changing.
* Recover immediately if you make a mistake.

# 🎹 2. 3-Chord Songs — I-IV-V

This is one of the most important practical skills.

In C major:

**I = C**
**IV = F**
**V = G**

So practice:

> C → F → G → C

### Practice Session

**Step 1 — Individual chords**

Play:

> C → F → G → C

Slowly, making each chord clean.

**Step 2 — One chord per bar**

Use 4/4:

> | C | F | G | C |

Repeat 10 times.

**Step 3 — Random starting point**

Don't always start with C.

Try:

> F → G → C → F

Then:

> G → C → F → G

This forces your brain to understand the **function**, rather than memorizing one sequence.

### 🎵 Song Application

Take a simple song you know and identify:

* Where does I occur?
* Where does IV occur?
* Where does V occur?

Then play only the chords while singing/humming the melody.

### ✅ Learning outcome

You should begin thinking:

**“This sounds like I → IV → V”**

instead of:

**“I have memorized these three chords.”**

---

# 🎹 3. 4-Chord Songs — I-V-vi-IV

This progression is extremely useful for modern songs.

In C major:

> **C → G → Am → F**

Practice this progression extensively.

### Practice Session

**Round 1 — Block chords**

Play:

> | C | G | Am | F |

Each chord = 4 beats.

Repeat **10 times**.

**Round 2 — Faster**

Increase gradually:

> 60 BPM → 70 BPM → 80 BPM → 90 BPM

Don't increase speed if your chord changes become messy.

**Round 3 — Rhythm**

Try:

> ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓

Then:

> ↓ ↓↑ ↑↓↑

### 🎵 Song challenge

Choose one simple song using this progression.

First play only chords.

Then add the rhythm.

Then try to sing/hum the melody.

### 🎯 Target

Eventually you should hear:

> C → G → Am → F

and immediately recognize:

> **I → V → vi → IV**

That is functional harmony thinking.

# 🎹 4. Chord + Melody

This is where your keyboard playing starts becoming much more musical.

Your **left hand** provides harmony.

Your **right hand** plays melody.

For example:

LH:

> C chord

RH:

> C D E G E

Then:

LH:

> G chord

RH:

> G A B D B

### Practice Session

**Step 1 — Separate hands**

Practice the melody alone.

Then practice the chords alone.

**Step 2 — Combine**

Play the chord with your left hand while playing only the first melody phrase with your right.

**Step 3 — Small sections**

Don't attempt an entire song.

Practice:

> 2 bars → stop
> 2 bars → stop
> 4 bars → stop

Then connect them.

### 🎯 Important rule

**Don't sacrifice rhythm because you're trying to play the melody perfectly.**

If necessary:

1. Simplify the melody.
2. Slow down.
3. Keep the chord pulse steady.

### ✅ Target

You should eventually be able to:

**LH = automatic chord accompaniment**

while

**RH = independent melody**

---

# 🥁 5. Chord Rhythm Patterns

Knowing chords isn't enough.

You need to make them sound like **music**.

Take:

> C → G → Am → F

and play it using several rhythmic patterns.

### Pattern A — Basic

> ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓

Four beats per bar.

### Pattern B — Pop

> ↓ ↓↑ ↑↓↑

### Pattern C — Slow

> ↓ — ↓↑ — ↑

### Pattern D — Sustained

> ↓ — — — | ↓ — — —

### Practice Session

Choose **one chord progression**.

Play it for 2 minutes using Pattern A.

Then:

2 minutes → Pattern B
2 minutes → Pattern C
2 minutes → Pattern D

### 🎯 Important

Don't change chords randomly.

Keep the chord progression exactly the same.

Only change the **rhythm**.

This trains your brain to separate:

**Harmony = chord**

from

**Accompaniment = rhythm.**

# 🎹 6. Inversion-Based Accompaniment

This is extremely important for making your playing sound smooth.

Instead of always playing:

> C = C E G
> G = G B D
> Am = A C E
> F = F A C

you can use nearby inversions.

For example:

> C = C E G
> G/B = B D G
> Am = A C E
> F/A = A C F

Your hand doesn't have to jump around as much.

### Practice Session

First play:

> C → G → Am → F

using root-position chords.

Then find the **closest inversion** for each next chord.

Your goal is:

> **minimum hand movement**

### 🎯 Exercise

Play the progression extremely slowly.

After every chord ask:

**“Can I play the next chord without moving my hand far?”**

### Learning outcome

Your accompaniment becomes:

* smoother
* faster
* more professional
* easier to play
* less physically tiring

This skill will be especially useful in your contest.

# 🔄 7. Transpose a Song

Now take a progression you already know.

For example:

> C → G → Am → F

This is:

> I → V → vi → IV

Now transpose it to G major.

G major:

> I = G
> V = D
> vi = Em
> IV = C

So:

> **G → D → Em → C**

Then try A major:

> **A → E → F#m → D**

### Practice Session

Take one song.

Play it in:

1. C major
2. G major
3. D major
4. A major

Don't memorize every chord separately.

Think:

> **I → V → vi → IV**

Then calculate the chords in the new key.

### 🎯 Challenge

Pick a random key.

Give yourself **10 seconds**.

Then identify:

> I, IV, V, vi

and play them.

# 👀 8. Play Without Looking

This connects directly with your **keyboard geography** training.

The goal is to stop depending on your eyes.

### Practice Session

Start very slowly.

Look at the chord.

Play it.

Then look away.

Play the same chord again.

For example:

> C → look away → C

> F → look away → F

> G → look away → G

Then:

> C → F → G → C

without looking.

### Level progression

**Level 1**

Don't look at your right hand.

**Level 2**

Don't look at your left hand.

**Level 3**

Don't look at either hand.

**Level 4**

Play a complete progression without looking.

### 🎯 Important

Don't close your eyes immediately.

First develop:

**touch → hand position → keyboard geography → muscle memory**

# 🎲 9. Random Chord Challenge

This is your **instant recall test**.

Create random combinations such as:

> C major
> F minor
> A major
> D minor
> G major

Then immediately play them.

### Practice Session

Give yourself **5 seconds per chord** initially.

Example:

> 🎲 D major → PLAY
> 🎲 Am → PLAY
> 🎲 F major → PLAY
> 🎲 C minor → PLAY

Then reduce the response time.

### Next level

Randomize:

**Root + quality**

Examples:

> Bb major
> F# minor
> E major
> C minor
> Ab major

### Advanced version

Randomize:

**Root + quality + inversion**

For example:

> E major, 1st inversion

You should be able to construct it quickly.

### 🎯 Contest target

Eventually:

**Hear/see → recognize → form chord → play**

with almost no conscious delay.

# 🏆 10. Contest Simulation

This is the final boss of Bucket 5.10.

You combine everything you've learned.

### 🎯 Simulation Format

Create a **10-round test**.

For each round:

### Round 1–3: Chord recognition

Look at a chord name.

Example:

> **D major**

You have **3–5 seconds** to play it.

---

### Round 4–5: Progression

You receive:

> C → G → Am → F

Play it continuously.

No stopping.

---

### Round 6: Rhythm

Play the progression using a specified rhythm.

Example:

> C → G → Am → F
> Rhythm: ↓ ↓↑ ↑↓↑

---

### Round 7: Inversions

Play:

> C → G → Am → F

using smooth nearby inversions.

---

### Round 8: Melody + Chord

Play the chord progression with your LH while playing a simple melody with your RH.

---

### Round 9: Transposition

You are given:

> C → G → Am → F

Then told:

> **Transpose to G**

You must produce:

> G → D → Em → C

---

### Round 10: Blind/Instant Challenge

You hear or see a chord.

Immediately:

> **Identify → Find → Play**

No preparation.
