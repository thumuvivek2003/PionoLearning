# 🎼 LEVEL 7 — 🎹 PLAYING PATTERNS

## 🎵 Bucket 7.5 — Chord + Melody

### 🎯 Overall Goal

By the end of 7.5, you should be able to:

**Left Hand → Chords / Harmony**
**Right Hand → Melody**
**Both Hands → One Musical Performance**

Start very slowly. Accuracy and coordination are more important than speed.

---

## 🎹 7.5.1 — Single Chord + Melody

### 🎯 Objective

Learn the basic idea of **accompanying a melody with one chord**.

### Step 1 — Choose C Major

Left hand:

**C–E–G**

Hold the C chord.

Right hand:

Play a very simple melody using:

**C – D – E – D – C**

### Step 2 — Hold the chord

Play:

**LH:** C–E–G → hold

While holding it:

**RH:** C → D → E → D → C

Don't worry about rhythm initially.

### Step 3 — Try different melodies

Keep the same C chord while playing simple melodies.

For example:

**RH:** C–E–G–E–D–C

Then:

**RH:** E–F–G–E–D–C

### Step 4 — Change the rhythm

Once comfortable, try:

**LH:** C–E–G → hold

**RH:** ♪ ♪ ♪ ♪

Then:

**LH:** C–E–G → hold

**RH:** ♩ ♪ ♪ ♩

### 🎯 What you should feel

Your left hand should feel **stable**, while your right hand moves freely.

### ✅ Mastery Test

You are ready to move on when you can:

* Hold the C chord without looking at your left hand.
* Play a simple 5–8 note melody with your right hand.
* Keep a steady tempo.
* Avoid stopping when the melody changes notes.

---

# 🎹 7.5.2 — Block Chord + Melody

Now we make the left hand more active.

### 🎯 Objective

Play **block chords with the left hand** while the right hand plays melody.

Instead of holding:

**C →————**

you will play:

**C chord → C chord → C chord → C chord**

### Step 1 — Practice the chord alone

LH:

**C–E–G | C–E–G | C–E–G | C–E–G**

Play once per beat.

Count:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 4**

### Step 2 — Add melody

LH:

**C chord | C chord | C chord | C chord**

RH:

**C | D | E | G**

### Step 3 — Make a 4-bar exercise

Count:

**1 2 3 4**

LH plays C chord on every count.

RH plays:

**C – D – E – G**

Then:

**G – E – D – C**

### Step 4 — Practice without looking

Your goal is to stop visually checking both hands.

Look mostly at the **right hand**, while the left hand becomes automatic.

### 🎯 Mastery Test

Play 4–8 measures where:

* LH plays repeated C block chords.
* RH plays a simple melody.
* No stopping.
* Both hands remain rhythmically synchronized.

---

# 🎹 7.5.3 — C Chord + Melody

Now we specifically train **melodies that work over C Major**.

### 🎯 Objective

Understand that a chord creates a **harmonic environment** for the melody.

C Major contains:

**C – D – E – F – G – A – B**

### Step 1 — Left hand

Play:

**C–E–G**

### Step 2 — Right hand

Practice:

**C – D – E – G – E – D – C**

Then:

**E – F – G – A – G – F – E**

Then:

**C – E – G – E – D – C**

### Step 3 — Listen

Don't just play the notes.

Listen to how the melody sounds **over the C chord**.

Try ending the melody on:

**C**

Notice how stable it sounds.

Then end on:

**D**

Notice how it feels less final.

This is your first practical introduction to **melody + harmony relationship**.

### Step 4 — Rhythm

Use a metronome around:

**♩ = 50–60 BPM**

Play one note per beat initially.

### 🎯 Mastery Test

You should be able to improvise a simple 8-note melody using the C-major notes while maintaining the C chord underneath.

---

# 🎹 7.5.4 — C–F–G Chord + Melody

🔥 This is a major milestone.

You now introduce the three most important beginner chords:

**C Major → F Major → G Major**

### 🎯 Objective

Learn to play a melody while the harmony changes.

### Step 1 — Chord shapes

LH:

**C = C–E–G**

**F = F–A–C**

**G = G–B–D**

Practice changing:

**C → F → G → C**

until the changes are automatic.

### Step 2 — Add a simple melody

Example:

**C chord:** C – D – E – G

**F chord:** A – G – F – E

**G chord:** G – A – B – G

**C chord:** E – D – C

### Step 3 — Four-bar exercise

Think:

**Bar 1 → C**
**Bar 2 → F**
**Bar 3 → G**
**Bar 4 → C**

RH continuously plays the melody.

### Step 4 — Don't stop during chord changes

This is extremely important.

❌ Don't do:

Melody → STOP → change chord → Melody

Instead:

Melody → **change chord underneath** → Melody continues.

### 🎯 Mastery Test

Play:

**C → F → G → C**

while continuously playing a simple melody.

Your melody should **not stop when the chord changes**.

---

# 🎹 7.5.5 — Chord Changes + Melody

🔥 This is the real coordination exercise.

### 🎯 Objective

Make the left hand change chords **automatically while the right hand remains independent**.

### Step 1 — Chords alone

Practice:

**C | F | G | C**

Then:

**C | G | F | C**

Then:

**C | F | C | G**

### Step 2 — Add very simple melody

LH:

**C | F | G | C**

RH:

**C D E G | A G F E | G A B G | E D C**

### Step 3 — Slow tempo

Start:

**♩ = 40 BPM**

Do NOT increase speed until you can play without interruption.

### Step 4 — Make the melody easier

This is important.

If your brain is struggling, simplify the melody.

For example:

LH:

**C | F | G | C**

RH:

**E | F | G | E**

Then gradually make the melody more complicated.

### Step 5 — Increase tempo

Try:

**40 → 50 → 60 → 70 BPM**

Only increase when the previous speed is comfortable.

### 🎯 Mastery Test

You should be able to:

**Change 3 chords with LH**

while

**RH continues playing 8–16 notes**

without stopping.

---

# 🎹 7.5.6 — Melody Phrasing

🎵 Now we move from **mechanical playing → musical playing**.

### 🎯 Objective

Learn to make the melody expressive while the chord accompaniment stays controlled.

You will practice:

**Louder → Softer → Louder → Softer**

### Step 1 — Same melody, different dynamics

Play:

**C – D – E – G – E – D – C**

First:

**All equal volume**

Then:

**C – D – E → gradually louder**

**G → strongest**

**E – D – C → gradually softer**

### Step 2 — Keep chord softer

LH:

**C–E–G**

RH:

**C – D – E – G – E – D – C**

Your left hand should be **softer** than your melody.

Think:

> 🎹 LH = background
> 🎵 RH = singer

### Step 3 — Practice musical breathing

Divide your melody into phrases.

Example:

**C – D – E – G | E – D – C**

Imagine the first part is a musical sentence.

Then take a tiny musical breath.

Continue.

### Step 4 — Accentuate important notes

Don't accent every note.

Choose one important note.

For example:

**C – D – E – G – E – D – C**

Make **G** slightly stronger.

### 🎯 Mastery Test

Record yourself and ask:

> “Can I clearly hear the melody above the chord?”

If yes → excellent.

If the chord sounds louder than the melody → reduce LH volume.

# 🎹 7.5.7 — Simple Song Arrangement

🏆 This is the **final integration session** of Bucket 7.5.

### 🎯 Objective

Take everything you've learned and create a small musical arrangement.

You need:

**LH → Chords**

**RH → Melody**

**Rhythm → Steady**

**Dynamics → Musical**

### Step 1 — Choose a very easy song

Choose a song whose melody you already know.

Don't choose a difficult song.

The purpose here is **coordination**, not learning a difficult melody.

### Step 2 — Identify the chords

Start with:

**C – F – G – C**

Even if the original song uses more chords, simplify it initially.

### Step 3 — Practice LH alone

Play the chord progression repeatedly:

**C | F | G | C**

Until you don't need to think about the chord changes.

### Step 4 — Practice RH alone

Play the song melody.

Don't add chords yet.

Make sure you can play the melody smoothly.

### Step 5 — Combine

Now:

**LH:** C → F → G → C

**RH:** Song melody

Start extremely slowly.

### Step 6 — Add expression

Once the coordination works:

* Melody slightly louder
* Chords softer
* Phrase endings softer
* Important melody notes emphasized
* Avoid unnecessary accents

### Step 7 — Performance practice

Finally, play the entire passage **without stopping**.

If you make a small mistake:

❌ Don't restart.

Keep going.

This is especially important for your contest preparation.
