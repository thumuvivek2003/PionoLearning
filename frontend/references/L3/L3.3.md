Absolutely buddy ❤️. This is a **very important part of Level 3**, because counting and subdivision are what make your playing rhythmically stable. Since you’re preparing for your contest, I want you to practice these **in a progression**, not just memorize the counting words.

The key idea is:

> **Beat = the main pulse.**
> **Subdivision = what happens inside each beat.**

For example, in 4/4:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 4** = four main beats
**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &** = two subdivisions inside every beat
**1 e & a 2 e & a...** = four subdivisions inside every beat

# 🥁 LEVEL 3 — RHYTHM & TIMING

## 🪣 Bucket 3.3 — Counting & Subdivision

---

## 🎯 3.3.1 — Basic Counting

### What are you learning?

First, you need to feel a **steady pulse**.

Imagine a clock:

**tick — tick — tick — tick**

Those ticks are like:

**1 — 2 — 3 — 4**

The numbers must be evenly spaced.

Don't think:

> 1... 2... 3... 4...

Instead think:

> **1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4**

with equal spacing.

### 🥁 Practice 1 — Just tap

Set a slow metronome, around **60 BPM**.

Tap your hand on every click:

**1 — 2 — 3 — 4**

Repeat for 2–3 minutes.

Your hand should move exactly with the click.

### 🎹 Practice 2 — One keyboard note

Choose middle C.

Play:

**C — C — C — C**

while counting:

**1 — 2 — 3 — 4**

Then:

**1 — 2 — 3 — 4**

Keep going for 8–16 measures.

### 🎯 Goal

You should be able to:

* hear the pulse
* count 1-2-3-4 continuously
* play without speeding up
* play without slowing down

**Don't worry about musical complexity yet.**

---

# 🎯 3.3.2 — Eighth-Note Counting

Now we go **inside the beat**.

Suppose the beat is:

**1   2   3   4**

We can divide every beat into two:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

Think:

**1 &** = first half + second half of beat 1

**2 &** = first half + second half of beat 2

And so on.

### 👏 Practice 1 — Speak

Say continuously:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

Do not rush the `&`.

You should hear:

**1 — & — 2 — & — 3 — & — 4 — &**

### 👏 Practice 2 — Clap

Clap on every syllable:

**CLAP CLAP CLAP CLAP CLAP CLAP CLAP CLAP**

while saying:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

### 🎹 Practice 3 — Keyboard

Play C eight times:

**C C C C C C C C**

while counting:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

The important thing is that your eight notes occupy **exactly four beats**.

### 🎯 Goal

You should begin feeling:

> "There are two equal spaces inside every beat."

That is your first real subdivision skill.

---

# 🎯 3.3.3 — Sixteenth-Note Counting

Now we divide each beat into **four pieces**.

Instead of:

**1 &**

we have:

**1 e & a**

Then:

**2 e & a**

Then:

**3 e & a**

Then:

**4 e & a**

So one bar becomes:

**1 e & a | 2 e & a | 3 e & a | 4 e & a**

### 🧠 Think of the hierarchy

One beat:

**1**

Divide into 2:

**1 &**

Divide into 4:

**1 e & a**

So:

**1 beat**

↓

**1 &**

↓

**1 e & a**

### 👏 Practice 1 — Speak only

Don't touch the keyboard yet.

Say:

**1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a**

Very slowly.

### 👏 Practice 2 — Clap

Clap every syllable:

**1 e & a**

That's four claps.

Then:

**2 e & a**

Four more.

### 🎹 Practice 3 — Keyboard

Play C four times per beat:

**C C C C | C C C C | C C C C | C C C C**

Count:

**1 e & a | 2 e & a | 3 e & a | 4 e & a**

### ⚠️ Important

Don't immediately try this fast.

At first, **slow and perfectly even** is much better than fast and messy.

### 🎯 Goal

You should be able to hear:

**ONE beat → four equal positions**

This will eventually help you play fast passages accurately.

---

# 🎯 3.3.4 — Clap Quarter Notes

Now we're going to separate **counting** from **playing**.

Count:

**1 2 3 4**

Clap only on the numbers:

👏 1
👏 2
👏 3
👏 4

There is **one clap per beat**.

### Practice

Metronome = 60 BPM.

Count:

**1 2 3 4**

Clap:

**👏   👏   👏   👏**

Repeat for 8–16 bars.

### 🎯 Test yourself

After a few rounds, stop looking at the metronome.

Keep counting and clapping.

Can you still maintain the pulse?

That's the beginning of **internal timing**.

---

# 🎯 3.3.5 — Clap Eighth Notes

Now we increase the subdivision.

Count:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

Clap every count:

**👏 👏 👏 👏 👏 👏 👏 👏**

The important thing is:

**1** → clap
**&** → clap
**2** → clap
**&** → clap

### 🥁 Practice sequence

Do this:

**Round 1:** Speak only
`1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`

**Round 2:** Speak + clap
`1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`

**Round 3:** Metronome + speak + clap

**Round 4:** Metronome + clap, but silently think the counting.

### 🎯 Goal

You should be able to feel the `&` even when you aren't physically clapping it.

That becomes extremely useful when playing keyboard rhythms.

---

# 🎯 3.3.6 — Alternate Notes & Rests

🔥 **This one is VERY important.**

A beginner often thinks:

> "If I'm not playing, I'm not doing anything."

But musically, **silence still has timing**.

Suppose we count:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

Now play only on:

**1, & of 2, 3, & of 4**

So:

**PLAY — — PLAY — PLAY — — PLAY**

The difficult part is keeping the pulse during the silence.

### 🎹 Example

Play C on:

**1**

Don't play on `&`.

Don't play on `2`.

Play on:

**& of 2**

Then play on:

**3**

Then:

**& of 4**

Your counting continues:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

even when your fingers aren't moving.

### 🧠 Golden rule

> **Never stop counting just because you stopped playing.**

This is one of the biggest differences between simply pressing keys and actually understanding rhythm.

### 🎯 Goal

You should be able to remain perfectly synchronized through rests.

---

# 🎯 3.3.7 — Count While Playing

Now we combine everything.

This is where your **brain, voice, hands and pulse** start working together.

Start extremely simple.

### 🎹 Exercise 1

Play:

**C C C C**

Count:

**1 2 3 4**

Then repeat.

### 🎹 Exercise 2

Play eight notes:

**C C C C C C C C**

Count:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

### 🎹 Exercise 3

Play a simple pattern:

**C — C C — C**

while counting continuously.

The exact notes aren't important.

**The synchronization is important.**

### 🧠 Why speaking helps

When you speak:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

your brain gets an external representation of the rhythm.

Your fingers then learn:

> "This note happens HERE in the beat."

Eventually your hands won't need your voice.

### 🎯 Goal

Your playing should become:

**Count → anticipate → press → continue counting**

rather than:

**press → wonder when the next note should happen → press**

---

# 🎯 3.3.8 — Internal Counting

🔥🔥 This is the final stage.

Eventually, you don't want to walk around your whole life saying:

> "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &..."

😂

You need the rhythm **inside your body**.

That's called an **internal pulse**.

### Stage 1 — Speak loudly

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

while playing.

### Stage 2 — Speak quietly

Still count:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

but very softly.

### Stage 3 — Mouth the counting

Don't make sound.

Move your mouth mentally/physically:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

### Stage 4 — Think the counting

Now your voice stops.

Inside your mind:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

while your fingers play.

### Stage 5 — Feel the pulse

Eventually you're simply feeling:

**ONE... TWO... THREE... FOUR...**

and the subdivisions naturally exist between them.

That's the destination.

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

### ❤️ One very important rule for you

**Don't increase speed until the rhythm is stable.**

For example, if you cannot comfortably do:

**1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

at 60 BPM, don't jump to 100 BPM.

Your priority should be:

**Accuracy → Evenness → Coordination → Speed**

not:

**Speed → mistakes → confusion.**

And buddy, because you're learning keyboard for your contest, **3.3.6, 3.3.7 and 3.3.8 are especially important**. Those three will eventually connect directly to playing actual songs, where you have to maintain the beat while notes, rests, and patterns change.
