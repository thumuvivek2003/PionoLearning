# LEVEL 5 — 🎶 CHORDS & HARMONY

## Bucket 5.5 — 🎼 7th Chords

### 🎯 Final Goal

By the end of this bucket, you should be able to see:

**Cmaj7 → C-E-G-B**

and immediately play it without calculating every note.

Similarly:

**C7 → C-E-G-B♭**

And eventually:

**F#m7 → F#-A-C#-E**

# 🟢 SESSION 1 — What is a 7th Chord?

### 🎯 Objective

Understand why a 7th chord has **4 notes** instead of the 3 notes of a triad.

### Step 1 — Review a triad

Take C major:

**C-E-G**

This is:

**1-3-5**

Now add the 7th note:

**C-E-G-B**

This becomes:

**1-3-5-7**

That's a **C Major 7 chord (Cmaj7).**

### Step 2 — Physically play it

Play:

**C → E → G → B**

with your right hand.

Then play:

**C → E → G → B → G → E → C**

Slowly.

### Step 3 — Compare

Play:

**C-E-G**

Then:

**C-E-G-B**

Listen carefully.

The second chord should sound more **open, colorful and sophisticated**.

### Step 4 — Keyboard recognition

Don't think too much about theory.

Practice:

**C major → Cmaj7**

**G major → Gmaj7**

**F major → Fmaj7**

### 🎯 Target

You should understand:

> **Triad = 1-3-5**
> **7th chord = 1-3-5 + 7th**

### ⏱ Practice

**10–15 minutes**

# 🟢 SESSION 2 — Major 7 Chords

### Formula

**1-3-5-7**

Major 7 has:

> Major triad + major 7th

Example:

**Cmaj7**

C major:

**C-E-G**

**C-E-G-B**

**3. Say the notes**

**4. Play it**

**5. Release**

**6. Play again**

Do this slowly.

# 🟢 SESSION 3 — Dominant 7 Chords

This is **very important** for keyboard playing.

### Formula

**1-3-5-♭7**

Example:

### C7

C major:

**C-E-G**

Lower the 7th:

B → B♭

Therefore:

**C-E-G-B♭**

---

### Compare

**Cmaj7**

C-E-G-B

versus

**C7**

C-E-G-B♭

That single note changes the chord's character.

---

### Practice

| Chord | Notes    |
| ----- | -------- |
| C7    | C-E-G-B♭ |
| F7    | F-A-C-E♭ |
| G7    | G-B-D-F  |
| D7    | D-F#-A-C |
| A7    | A-C#-E-G |

### 🎯 Exercise

Play:

**C → C7**

**F → F7**

**G → G7**

Notice how the 7th chord creates more **tension**.

Then try:

**G7 → C**

This is particularly important because **G7 naturally wants to resolve to C**.

---

### 🔥 Contest exercise

Play:

**C → G7 → C**

Then:

**F → C7 → F**

Then:

**G → D7 → G**

This starts connecting your chord knowledge to actual musical progressions.

### ⏱ Practice

**15–20 minutes**

# 🟡 SESSION 9 — 7th Chord Inversions

Now we take:

**C-E-G-B**

and move notes around.

### Root position

**C-E-G-B**

### 1st inversion

Move C to the top:

**E-G-B-C**

### 2nd inversion

Move E to the top:

**G-B-C-E**

### 3rd inversion

Move G to the top:

**B-C-E-G**

So:

```text
Cmaj7

Root position
C-E-G-B

1st inversion
E-G-B-C

2nd inversion
G-B-C-E

3rd inversion
B-C-E-G
```

---

### Practice slowly

Play:

**C-E-G-B**

↓

**E-G-B-C**

↓

**G-B-C-E**

↓

**B-C-E-G**

↓

Back to:

**C-E-G-B**

Do this **5–10 cycles**.

# 🎹 Your Daily Practice Pattern

For your contest preparation, buddy, I **wouldn't spend huge amounts of time on 7th chords yet**.

Use this structure:

```text
LEVEL 5.5 — 7TH CHORD DAILY PRACTICE

1. Review formulas             3 min
2. Build chords                5 min
3. Root-position practice      5 min
4. Chord comparison            5 min
5. Inversion practice          5 min
6. Random-speed drill           5 min

TOTAL                          25–30 min
```

And one important point for your **45-day contest**:

**Don't sacrifice speed and accuracy on major/minor triads and their inversions just to learn 7th chords.** Your triads should become almost automatic first. Then 7th chords become much easier because you're essentially taking a familiar triad and adding/modifying one note.

### 🔥 The progression I want you to follow

```text
Major Triads
      ↓
Minor Triads
      ↓
Major/Minor Inversions
      ↓
7th Chord Formulas
      ↓
Maj7 / 7 / m7
      ↓
7th Chord Inversions
      ↓
Random 7th Chord Drills
      ↓
Fast Chord Recognition
```

That is the path I'd use for your contest preparation. 🎹🔥
