# LEVEL 5 — 🎶 CHORDS & HARMONY

## Bucket 5.4 — 🔄 Chord Inversions

### 🎯 Main Goal

By the end of this bucket, when you see:

**C → G → Am → F**

you should NOT automatically think:

> C root → G root → Am root → F root

Instead, you should think:

> **“Which position of the next chord is closest to where my hand already is?”**

That's the beginning of real chord accompaniment. 🎹

---

# 🟢 Session 1 — Root Position

### What you learn

A triad in **root position** has the root note at the bottom.

For C major:

**C – E – G**

* C = Root
* E = Major 3rd
* G = Perfect 5th

### Practice

Start with:

**C major → C-E-G**

Play it slowly with your right hand:

**1 – 3 – 5**

Then release and play it again.

Do this 10 times.

Then practice other major chords:

* C = C-E-G
* D = D-F#-A
* E = E-G#-B
* F = F-A-C
* G = G-B-D
* A = A-C#-E

### Drill

Look at a chord name and immediately find the **root-position shape**.

For example:

> G

Your fingers should immediately find:

**G-B-D**

### 🎯 Goal

You should be able to play the major triads without having to calculate every note.

**Target:** 8/10 correct without hesitation.

---

# 🟢 Session 2 — 1st Inversion

Now we start moving the notes.

C major:

**Root:** C-E-G

Move the **lowest note C** to the top:

**E-G-C**

That's **1st inversion**.

Think:

> C-E-G → E-G-C

### Practice slowly

Play:

**C-E-G**

Then:

**E-G-C**

Then:

**C-E-G**

Then:

**E-G-C**

Repeat 10 times.

### Important concept

The chord is STILL **C major**.

You haven't changed the chord.

You've only changed the **order of the notes**.

### Practice with other chords

For example:

**F major**

Root:

**F-A-C**

1st inversion:

**A-C-F**

**G major**

Root:

**G-B-D**

1st inversion:

**B-D-G**

### 🎯 Goal

When you hear:

> “C major, first inversion”

your hand should immediately know:

**E-G-C**

---

# 🟢 Session 3 — 2nd Inversion

Now move the lowest note of the 1st inversion to the top.

C major:

**C-E-G**

↓ 1st inversion

**E-G-C**

↓ 2nd inversion

**G-C-E**

So remember:

> **Root → 1st → 2nd**

**C-E-G → E-G-C → G-C-E**

### Practice cycle

Play:

**C-E-G**

**E-G-C**

**G-C-E**

**C-E-G**

Repeat 10 times.

Don't stop between positions.

The goal is to make the movement feel natural.

### 🎯 Goal

You should eventually be able to play:

**Root → 1st → 2nd → Root**

without thinking about individual notes.

---

# 🟢 Session 4 — Inversion Recognition

Now DON'T play immediately.

I want you to develop your **eyes + brain recognition**.

I'll give you three shapes:

### C-E-G

Root position → **ROOT**

### E-G-C

1st inversion → **1ST**

### G-C-E

2nd inversion → **2ND**

Now randomly test yourself.

For example:

> G-C-E

You should immediately say:

**“C major — 2nd inversion.”**

Another:

> E-G-C

Answer:

**“C major — 1st inversion.”**

### 🎯 Practice

Take 10 random triad shapes.

For each one identify:

1. Chord name
2. Major/minor
3. Inversion

For example:

**E-G-C**

Answer:

> C major — 1st inversion.

### 🎯 Goal

Recognition should become **instant**, not something you calculate slowly.

# 🟢 Session 7 — Inversion Switching

Now we make it musical.

Instead of practicing:

> Root → 1st → 2nd

as an isolated exercise, start **switching positions**.

For C major:

**Root → 1st → 2nd → Root**

C-E-G
↓
E-G-C
↓
G-C-E
↓
C-E-G

Then try:

**Root → 2nd → 1st → Root**

C-E-G
↓
G-C-E
↓
E-G-C
↓
C-E-G

Then random:

**1st → 2nd → Root → 1st**

### 🎯 Important

Try to **look at the shape**, rather than looking at each individual key.

Eventually your brain should recognize:

> “That's C major 2nd inversion.”

rather than:

> “G... C... E... okay, that's C major.”

# 🟢 Session 8 — Same Chord, Different Position

This session is about understanding something VERY important.

Play:

**C-E-G**

Then:

**E-G-C**

Then:

**G-C-E**

Ask yourself:

> Did the chord change?

**NO.**

All three are:

### C Major

The only thing that changed is the **lowest note/order**.

---

### Listen carefully

Play each one slowly.

Notice that they have different **colors/feelings**, but they still sound like C major.

This is extremely important for accompaniment.

For example:

**C-E-G**

sounds more grounded.

**E-G-C**

has a different character.

**G-C-E**

can feel more open/high.

### 🎯 Exercise

Play:

**C major root**

Hold it for 2 seconds.

Then:

**C major 1st inversion**

Hold it for 2 seconds.

Then:

**C major 2nd inversion**

Hold it for 2 seconds.

Listen carefully.

### 🎯 Goal

Your ears should eventually recognize:

> Same chord, different position.

# 🟢 Session 9 — Minimal Movement Drill ⭐

🔥 **THIS is one of the most important sessions in the entire bucket.**

Now we connect inversions to real music.

Suppose you have:

**C → G**

If you play both in root position:

**C-E-G**

then:

**G-B-D**

your hand has to move significantly.

But instead, look for the closest G inversion.

From:

**C-E-G**

you can move to:

**B-D-G**

That's G major **1st inversion**.

Now your hand moves much less.

---

### Example

C major:

**C-E-G**

G major:

**B-D-G**

Notice how close the notes are.

This is called **voice leading**.

You're basically asking:

> “How can I move the individual notes the smallest possible distance?”

🔥 This is the skill that makes chord playing sound smooth.

# 🔥 BONUS — The Real Musical Drill

Once you've completed all 10 sessions, THIS is where I want you to spend serious time.

Take:

**C → G → Am → F**

First play everything in root position:

**C-E-G**

→ **G-B-D**

→ **A-C-E**

→ **F-A-C**

Now try to find the **nearest inversion** for each next chord.

For example, you might use:

**C-E-G**

→ **B-D-G**

→ **A-C-E**

→ **A-C-F**

Notice what's happening.

Your hand is no longer jumping all over the keyboard.

You're keeping the notes **close together**.

That's the whole purpose of inversions.

# 🧠 Your Mental Model

I want you to remember this simple system:

### Root Position

**Root – 3rd – 5th**

### 1st Inversion

**3rd – 5th – Root**

### 2nd Inversion

**5th – Root – 3rd**

So for ANY triad:

```text
ROOT POSITION
1 - 3 - 5

1ST INVERSION
3 - 5 - 1

2ND INVERSION
5 - 1 - 3
```

This pattern works for both **major and minor triads**.
