Absolutely buddy 🎹. Since **Alberti Bass** is an accompaniment pattern, the main goal is not just to memorize `1–5–3–5`, but to make your **left hand automatic, even, and steady** while your right hand can play a melody.

For your 45-day contest preparation, I’d practice these **6 sessions in order**.

---

# 🎼 LEVEL 7 — 🎹 PLAYING PATTERNS

## 🎹 Bucket 7.4 — Alberti Bass

### 🎯 Overall Goal

By the end of this bucket, you should be able to:

**See a chord → immediately find 1–5–3–5 → play it evenly → change chords without stopping → play a melody with your right hand.**

---

## 🎹 7.4.1 — Alberti Bass Pattern

### 🎯 Objective

Understand and physically memorize the classic:

**1 → 5 → 3 → 5**

For a C Major chord:

**C → G → E → G**

### Step 1 — Know the chord

C Major:

**C – E – G**

Number them:

* 1 = C
* 3 = E
* 5 = G

Now rearrange them as:

**1 – 5 – 3 – 5**

So:

**C – G – E – G**

### Step 2 — Play very slowly

Use your **left hand**.

Try:

**C G E G | C G E G | C G E G | C G E G**

Don't worry about speed.

Focus on:

> **Even → relaxed → even → relaxed**

### Step 3 — Use a metronome

Start around **♩ = 50–60 BPM**.

Play one note per beat:

**C → G → E → G**

Count:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 4**

Then repeat.

### Step 4 — Remove visual dependence

Look at the C chord once.

Then try playing:

**C G E G**

without constantly looking at your hand.

### 🏆 Move on when

You can play **8–16 repetitions** without:

* stopping
* hitting wrong notes
* changing rhythm
* becoming tense

---

# 🎹 7.4.2 — C Major Alberti Bass

### 🎯 Objective

Make **C–G–E–G** automatic.

Now we're going to turn the basic pattern into a real accompaniment exercise.

### Pattern

**C – G – E – G | C – G – E – G**

### Exercise A — Individual notes

Play:

**C G E G**

Rest.

Repeat.

Do this for about **2 minutes**.

### Exercise B — Continuous

Play:

**C G E G | C G E G | C G E G | C G E G**

for **8 measures**.

Keep the pulse absolutely steady.

### Exercise C — Change the octave

Try the same pattern in another C position:

**C G E G**

higher on the keyboard.

This teaches your hand that Alberti Bass is a **shape/pattern**, not just four memorized keys.

### Exercise D — Dynamics

Play one repetition softly:

**C G E G**

Then slightly louder:

**C G E G**

Then softly again.

This helps you avoid a robotic sound.

### 🏆 Target

You should eventually be able to play C Major Alberti Bass for **1–2 minutes continuously** without breaking the pulse.

---

# 🎹 7.4.3 — F Major Alberti Bass

### 🎯 Objective

Transfer exactly the same concept to a new chord.

F Major:

**F – A – C**

Therefore:

**1 – 5 – 3 – 5**

becomes:

**F – C – A – C**

### Step 1 — Memorize

Say:

> **F → C → A → C**

Then play it.

### Step 2 — Slow practice

**F C A C | F C A C | F C A C | F C A C**

Start at around **50–60 BPM**.

### Step 3 — Compare with C Major

C Major:

**C – G – E – G**

F Major:

**F – C – A – C**

Notice something important:

The **pattern doesn't change**.

Only the chord changes.

That's the skill you're developing.

### Step 4 — C → F

Now alternate:

**C G E G | F C A C**

Repeat.

Then:

**C G E G | F C A C | C G E G | F C A C**

### 🏆 Target

You should be able to change between **C Major and F Major without stopping**.

---

# 🎹 7.4.4 — G Major Alberti Bass

### 🎯 Objective

Add G Major and build your first useful three-chord Alberti Bass progression.

G Major:

**G – B – D**

Therefore:

**1 – 5 – 3 – 5**

becomes:

**G – D – B – D**

### Step 1 — Practice G alone

**G D B D | G D B D | G D B D**

### Step 2 — C → G

**C G E G | G D B D**

Repeat several times.

### Step 3 — G → F

**G D B D | F C A C**

### Step 4 — C → F → G → C

This is the important exercise.

Play:

**C G E G | F C A C | G D B D | C G E G**

Repeat.

You are now practicing **chord changes with a continuous accompaniment pattern**.

### 🧠 Important

Don't think:

> "C chord... now find C G E G."

Instead, gradually train your brain to think:

> **C → pattern**

> **F → pattern**

> **G → pattern**

That is what will eventually make your playing fast.

### 🏆 Target

Play:

**C → F → G → C**

for **8–16 cycles** without stopping.

---

# 🎹 7.4.5 — Continuous Alberti Bass

### 🎯 Objective

Now we stop thinking about individual exercises and start thinking like an accompanist.

Your job is to maintain a **steady left-hand engine**.

### Exercise A — One chord for 8 measures

C Major:

**C G E G | C G E G | C G E G | C G E G**

Continue for **8 measures**.

Do not stop between measures.

### Exercise B — Four-chord loop

Use:

**C → F → G → C**

Play:

**C G E G | F C A C | G D B D | C G E G**

Repeat.

### Exercise C — Metronome

Start:

**60 BPM**

Then:

**70 BPM**

Then:

**80 BPM**

Don't increase the tempo until the pattern is clean.

### Exercise D — Accent control

Try making the **first note of every group of four** slightly stronger:

**C** G E G | **C** G E G

This helps you feel the four-note grouping.

Then remove the accent and make all four notes even.

### Exercise E — Longer endurance

Try:

**16 measures → 32 measures**

without stopping.

### 🏆 Target

You should be able to maintain the Alberti Bass pattern while your attention begins moving toward something else.

That is the bridge to **melody + accompaniment**.

# 🎹 7.4.6 — Alberti Bass with Melody

### 🎯 Objective

This is the **most important session** of the bucket.

Now combine:

**LEFT HAND → Alberti Bass**

*

**RIGHT HAND → Melody**

For example:

### Left Hand

**C G E G | C G E G**

### Right Hand

Play a very simple melody using only a few notes.

Don't start with a difficult song.

---

## 🥁 Step 1 — Lock the left hand

First play the Alberti Bass alone:

**C G E G | C G E G | C G E G | C G E G**

Make it automatic.

---

## 🎹 Step 2 — Add one right-hand note

While the left hand plays:

**C G E G**

hold a right-hand C.

So:

**LH:** C G E G
**RH:** C ————

Repeat.

This teaches your brain to separate the two hands.

---

## 🎹 Step 3 — Add a simple melody

For example:

**RH:** C D E D | C — — —

while:

**LH:** C G E G | C G E G

Don't worry if the melody is extremely simple.

The objective is **coordination**, not musical complexity.

---

## 🎹 Step 4 — Use C → F → G → C

Now play:

**LH**

C G E G | F C A C | G D B D | C G E G

while the right hand plays a simple melody.

### Your priority order should be:

**1. Correct notes**

↓

**2. Steady rhythm**

↓

**3. Smooth chord changes**

↓

**4. Melody**

↓

**5. Speed**

Never sacrifice the first four just to play faster.
