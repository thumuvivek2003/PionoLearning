# 4.8.1 — E Natural Minor Notes

### 🎯 Goal

Memorize:

**E – F# – G – A – B – C – D – E**

### Step 1 — Find E

On your keyboard, locate every **E**.

Remember:

**E is the white key immediately to the right of the group of two black keys.**

Don't start playing yet.

Just locate:

**E → F# → G → A → B → C → D → E**

### Step 2 — Say the notes aloud

Slowly say:

> E → F# → G → A → B → C → D → E

Then backward:

> E → D → C → B → A → G → F# → E

Do this several times.

### Step 3 — Play one octave

Play:

**E F# G A B C D E**

Then:

**E D C B A G F# E**

Don't worry about speed.

Your priority is:

**Correct notes > correct rhythm > speed**

### Step 4 — Random recall

Now don't look at the keyboard.

Ask yourself:

* What comes after E? → **F#**
* What comes after F#? → **G**
* What comes after G? → **A**
* What comes after B? → **C**
* What comes after C? → **D**
* What comes before E? → **D**

This is important because you want **instant recall**, not just memorization of the entire sequence.

### 🎵 Mini-test

Close your eyes and say:

**E → ? → ? → ? → ? → ? → ? → E**

You should be able to fill in:

**E → F# → G → A → B → C → D → E**

# 4.8.2 — E Minor Formula

Now understand **why** these notes are E minor.

The natural minor formula is:

**Whole – Half – Whole – Whole – Half – Whole – Whole**

Or:

**W – H – W – W – H – W – W**

Starting from E:

**E → F#** = Whole
**F# → G** = Half
**G → A** = Whole
**A → B** = Whole
**B → C** = Half
**C → D** = Whole
**D → E** = Whole

So:

**E – F# – G – A – B – C – D – E**

### 🎹 Keyboard exercise

Play very slowly while consciously checking the distances:

**E → F#**
Whole

**F# → G**
Half

**G → A**
Whole

**A → B**
Whole

**B → C**
Half

**C → D**
Whole

**D → E**
Whole

Don't just memorize the notes.

You want your brain to understand:

> **"E minor has this particular pattern of whole and half steps."**

### 🎯 Mini-test

Without looking at your notes:

**Natural minor formula = ?**

Answer:

**W – H – W – W – H – W – W**

Then ask:

> Starting from E, what is the third note?

**G**

That **G** is particularly important because it gives E minor its minor character.

# 4.8.3 — E Minor vs G Major

This is a **very important concept** for your Level 4.

### G Major

**G – A – B – C – D – E – F# – G**

### E Natural Minor

**E – F# – G – A – B – C – D – E**

Look carefully.

They contain exactly the same seven notes:

**G A B C D E F#**

The difference is the **starting/tonal center**.

G major feels like:

> **G is home.**

E minor feels like:

> **E is home.**

This relationship is called:

### 🎵 Relative Major / Relative Minor

**G Major ↔ E Minor**

And:

**E minor is the relative minor of G major.**

### 🎹 Practice

Play G major:

**G A B C D E F# G**

Stop on G.

Listen to the feeling of **G as home**.

Now play:

**E F# G A B C D E**

Stop on E.

Listen to the feeling of **E as home**.

This is a great exercise because you're training your **ear**, not just your fingers.

### 🧠 Recall test

I'll give you the major:

**G Major**

You answer:

**E Minor**

I'll give you the minor:

**E Minor**

You answer:

**G Major**

You should eventually answer almost instantly.

# 4.8.4 — Right Hand E Minor

Now we move into technique.

For **one octave ascending**, use:

**E(1) – F#(2) – G(3) – A(1) – B(2) – C(3) – D(4) – E(5)**

So the fingering is:

**1 2 3 | 1 2 3 4 5**

### 🎹 Ascending

Play:

**E(1)**
**F#(2)**
**G(3)**

Then tuck your thumb underneath:

**A(1)**

Continue:

**B(2)**
**C(3)**
**D(4)**
**E(5)**

### 🎹 Descending

Reverse it:

**E(5) – D(4) – C(3) – B(2) – A(1) – G(3) – F#(2) – E(1)**

Notice the important movement:

**A(1) → G(3)**

Your thumb passes underneath on the way up after G.

### Practice stages

**Round 1:** Extremely slow
Focus on correct fingers.

**Round 2:** Slow and even
Make every note the same volume.

**Round 3:** Slightly faster
Don't allow tension.

**Round 4:** Eyes away from your hand
Try to feel the geography.

### 🎯 Your standard

Don't increase speed until you can play:

**E–F#–G–A–B–C–D–E–D–C–B–A–G–F#–E**

without stopping.

# 4.8.5 — Left Hand E Minor

Now we train the left hand.

For one octave:

**E(5) – F#(4) – G(3) – A(2) – B(1) – C(3) – D(2) – E(1)**

So:

**5 4 3 2 1 | 3 2 1**

### 🎹 Ascending

Start:

**E(5)**
**F#(4)**
**G(3)**
**A(2)**
**B(1)**

Then cross:

**C(3)**
**D(2)**
**E(1)**

### 🎹 Descending

Reverse:

**E(1) – D(2) – C(3) – B(1) – A(2) – G(3) – F#(4) – E(5)**

### Important

Your left hand may feel less comfortable than your right hand.

That's completely normal.

Don't compensate by speeding up.

Instead:

**Slow → accurate → relaxed → consistent**

### 🎯 Special exercise

Play only:

**E F# G A B**

Stop.

Then:

**B C D E**

Stop.

Then combine them:

**E F# G A B C D E**

This isolates the thumb-crossing movement.

# 4.8.6 — Hands Together

Now comes the hardest part.

Don't immediately try to play the entire scale quickly.

First synchronize the notes.

Your hands should play the same notes at the same time.

### 🎹 Ascending

Right hand:

**1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5**

Left hand:

**5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1**

Together:

| Note | RH | LH |
| ---- | -: | -: |
| E    |  1 |  5 |
| F#   |  2 |  4 |
| G    |  3 |  3 |
| A    |  1 |  2 |
| B    |  2 |  1 |
| C    |  3 |  3 |
| D    |  4 |  2 |
| E    |  5 |  1 |

### Step 1 — Two notes

Play:

**E → F#**

Both hands.

Stop.

Check your fingers.

### Step 2 — Three notes

**E → F# → G**

Stop.

### Step 3 — Four notes

**E → F# → G → A**

Stop.

### Step 4 — Five notes

**E → F# → G → A → B**

Stop.

### Step 5 — Complete octave

**E → F# → G → A → B → C → D → E**

Then descend:

**E → D → C → B → A → G → F# → E**
