# 🎼 LEVEL 4 — SCALES & KEYBOARD PATTERNS

## 🪣 4.6 — Minor Scale Foundation

### 🎯 Overall Goal

By the end of 4.6, you should be able to:

> **Hear major vs minor → remember the natural-minor formula → find the relative minor → build any natural minor scale from a starting note.**

We'll break it into **5 practice sessions**.

---

# 🎹 4.6.1 — Major vs Minor Sound

### 🎯 Goal

First, train your **ear** to recognize that major and minor have different tonal characters.

Don't worry about formulas yet.

### Step 1 — Compare C Major and A Minor

Play:

**C Major**

> C – D – E – F – G – A – B – C

Then:

**A Natural Minor**

> A – B – C – D – E – F – G – A

Notice something interesting:

**They contain exactly the same white keys.**

But they sound different because the **tonal center** is different.

C Major feels like:

> **C is home.**

A Minor feels like:

> **A is home.**

---

### Step 2 — Listen for the character

Play each scale slowly.

Ask yourself:

**C Major**

* Does it feel brighter?
* Does it feel more open?
* Does it feel stable/happy?

**A Minor**

* Does it feel darker?
* Does it feel more serious?
* Does it feel more emotional?

Don't try to memorize "minor = sad."

That's an oversimplification.

Instead, train yourself to hear:

> **Major and minor have different tonal colors.**

---

### Step 3 — Ear Exercise

Play randomly:

1. C Major
2. A Minor
3. C Major
4. A Minor

Close your eyes.

Try to identify:

> **Major or Minor?**

Don't look at the keyboard while listening.

### 🎹 Practice Pattern

Do this for **5 minutes**.

Start with:

**C Major ↔ A Minor**

Then later:

**G Major ↔ E Minor**

**F Major ↔ D Minor**

The goal is not speed.

The goal is:

> **"I can hear the difference."**

---

# 🎹 4.6.2 — Natural Minor Formula

Now we introduce the most important formula.

## ⭐ Natural Minor Formula

**W – H – W – W – H – W – W**

Where:

**W = Whole Step = 2 semitones**

**H = Half Step = 1 semitone**

So the pattern is:

> **2 – 1 – 2 – 2 – 1 – 2 – 2**

---

## 🎹 Build A Minor

Start from:

**A**

### Step 1

A → B

Whole step ✅

### Step 2

B → C

Half step ✅

### Step 3

C → D

Whole step ✅

### Step 4

D → E

Whole step ✅

### Step 5

E → F

Half step ✅

### Step 6

F → G

Whole step ✅

### Step 7

G → A

Whole step ✅

Therefore:

**A – B – C – D – E – F – G – A**

---

## 🧠 Your Formula Memory

Don't memorize the notes of every minor scale first.

Memorize:

> **Natural Minor = W H W W H W W**

And convert it mentally to:

> **2 1 2 2 1 2 2**

That second version is extremely useful on the keyboard.

---

## 🎯 Practice Exercise

Pick a starting note.

For example:

**D**

Now calculate:

D → ?

Don't guess the scale from memory.

Use:

**W – H – W – W – H – W – W**

Build:

**D Natural Minor**

Then try:

**E Minor**

**G Minor**

**B Minor**

**C Minor**

Don't worry if accidentals appear.

That's actually the point of this exercise.

### 🎹 Target

You should eventually be able to hear:

> "Starting note → apply 2-1-2-2-1-2-2 → scale."

# 🎹 4.6.3 — Relative Minor Concept

This is where things become much easier.

A **relative minor** and a **major scale** contain the **same notes**, but have different tonal centers.

The classic example:

### C Major

**C – D – E – F – G – A – B**

### A Minor

**A – B – C – D – E – F – G**

Same notes.

Different home note.

Therefore:

> **A minor is the relative minor of C major.**

And:

> **C major is the relative major of A minor.**

---

## ⭐ The Important Relationship

Every major scale has a relative minor.

Every natural minor scale has a relative major.

### Example

| Major    | Relative Minor |
| -------- | -------------- |
| C Major  | A Minor        |
| G Major  | E Minor        |
| D Major  | B Minor        |
| A Major  | F♯ Minor       |
| E Major  | C♯ Minor       |
| F Major  | D Minor        |
| B♭ Major | G Minor        |

---

## 🎹 Keyboard Exercise

Play:

**C Major**

Then immediately play:

**A Minor**

Notice:

> Same seven pitch classes.

Then:

**G Major → E Minor**

Then:

**F Major → D Minor**

You're training your brain to see:

> **Major scale ↔ relative minor**

as a pair.

---

# 🎹 4.6.4 — Relative Minor From Major

Now we make the relationship extremely practical.

## ⭐ Rule

The relative minor starts on the:

> **6th degree of the major scale.**

For example:

### C Major

Number the notes:

**1  2  3  4  5  6  7**

**C  D  E  F  G  A  B**

The **6th degree = A**

Therefore:

> **C Major → A Minor**

---

## 🎹 Example 2 — G Major

G Major:

**G – A – B – C – D – E – F♯**

Number them:

1. G
2. A
3. B
4. C
5. D
6. **E**
7. F♯

Therefore:

> **G Major → E Minor**

---

## 🎹 Example 3 — F Major

F Major:

**F – G – A – B♭ – C – D – E**

6th degree:

**D**

Therefore:

> **F Major → D Minor**

---

## ⭐ Your Mental Shortcut

When someone asks:

> "What is the relative minor of G Major?"

Don't count semitones.

Don't guess.

Think:

**G Major**

↓
**1 2 3 4 5 6**

↓
**E**

Therefore:

> **E Minor**

---

# 🎹 4.6.5 — Minor Scale Construction

Now we combine everything.

This is the most important practical session.

Your job:

> **Given any starting note, construct its Natural Minor scale.**

---

## Example 1 — D Minor

Start:

**D**

Apply:

**W – H – W – W – H – W – W**

D → E = W

E → F = H

F → G = W

G → A = W

A → B♭ = H

B♭ → C = W

C → D = W

Therefore:

> **D – E – F – G – A – B♭ – C – D**

---

# Example 2 — E Minor

Start:

**E**

Apply the formula:

E → F♯ = W

F♯ → G = H

G → A = W

A → B = W

B → C = H

C → D = W

D → E = W

Therefore:

> **E – F♯ – G – A – B – C – D – E**

---

# Example 3 — G Minor

Start:

**G**

Apply:

W – H – W – W – H – W – W

You get:

> **G – A – B♭ – C – D – E♭ – F – G**

Notice that accidentals appear naturally.

**Don't be afraid of them.**

You're not memorizing them.

You're **calculating them**.

---

# 🧠 The Three Ways You Should Eventually Find a Minor Scale

By the end of 4.6, you should have **three mental methods**.

### Method 1 — Formula

**Starting note**

↓

**W H W W H W W**

↓

**Natural Minor**

---

### Method 2 — Relative Major

Find the relative major.

Then use its notes, starting from the minor's tonic.

Example:

**A Minor**

Relative major = **C Major**

C Major notes:

C D E F G A B

Start from A:

> **A B C D E F G**

---

### Method 3 — 6th Degree

Given a major scale:

> **6th degree = Relative Minor**

Example:

**D Major**

D E F♯ G A **B** C♯

6th degree = B

Therefore:

> **B Minor**

---

# 🎯 Your 4.6 Practice Routine

I recommend doing this over several days rather than trying to cram it.

### 🟢 Session A — Ear

**5–10 min**

Practice:

* C Major vs A Minor
* G Major vs E Minor
* F Major vs D Minor

Goal:

> Hear major vs minor character.

---

### 🟡 Session B — Formula

**10 min**

Write/say repeatedly:

> **W H W W H W W**

Then:

> **2 1 2 2 1 2 2**

Build 3–5 random minor scales.

---

### 🟠 Session C — Relative Minor

**10 min**

Take major scales and find their 6th degree.

For example:

**C → A**

**G → E**

**D → B**

**A → F♯**

**F → D**

**B♭ → G**

---

### 🔵 Session D — Keyboard Construction

**10–15 min**

Pick random starting notes.

For each one:

1. Find starting note.
2. Apply W-H-W-W-H-W-W.
3. Say each note aloud.
4. Play the scale.
5. Play it ascending.
6. Play it descending.

---

### 🔴 Session E — Random Challenge

This is your **test session**.

Don't look at notes.

Ask yourself:

> "Build B Natural Minor."

Then calculate it.

**B – C♯ – D – E – F♯ – G – A – B**

Next:

> "Build C Natural Minor."

**C – D – E♭ – F – G – A♭ – B♭ – C**

Next:

> "Build F♯ Natural Minor."

**F♯ – G♯ – A – B – C♯ – D – E – F♯**
