# 🪣 4.9.1 — D Natural Minor Notes

### 🎯 Objective

You should be able to say and locate every note of D minor **without hesitation**.

### Step 1 — Say the notes

Practice slowly:

**D → E → F → G → A → B♭ → C → D**

Then backwards:

**D → C → B♭ → A → G → F → E → D**

### Step 2 — Find them on the keyboard

Start from any **D**.

Then locate:

**D → E → F → G → A → B♭ → C → D**

Pay special attention to **B♭**.

Remember:

> **B♭ = the black key immediately to the LEFT of B.**

### Step 3 — Recognition drill

Randomly point to a note and ask yourself:

* "Is this part of D minor?"
* "What position is it in the scale?"

For example:

**F → 3rd note**

**A → 5th note**

**B♭ → 6th note**

**C → 7th note**

### 🕐 Practice

**5 minutes**

* 2 min: say notes
* 2 min: locate notes
* 1 min: random-note test

### ✅ You are ready when

You can look at a D and immediately identify:

**D E F G A B♭ C D**

without counting.

# 🪣 4.9.2 — D Minor Formula

### 🎯 Objective

Understand **how D minor is constructed**, instead of memorizing it as a sequence.

The natural minor formula is:

**Whole – Half – Whole – Whole – Half – Whole – Whole**

Or:

**W – H – W – W – H – W – W**

Apply it starting from **D**.

| Step | From → To | Distance |
| ---- | --------- | -------- |
| 1    | D → E     | Whole    |
| 2    | E → F     | Half     |
| 3    | F → G     | Whole    |
| 4    | G → A     | Whole    |
| 5    | A → B♭    | Half     |
| 6    | B♭ → C    | Whole    |
| 7    | C → D     | Whole    |

Therefore:

**D → E → F → G → A → B♭ → C → D**

### 🎹 Keyboard exercise

Don't immediately play the whole scale.

Instead play:

**D–E**

Stop.

**E–F**

Stop.

**F–G**

Stop.

**G–A**

Stop.

**A–B♭**

Stop.

**B♭–C**

Stop.

**C–D**

Stop.

This makes you physically feel the **whole-step/half-step pattern**.

### 🧠 Important discovery

The two half steps are:

**E → F**

and

**A → B♭**

These are very important landmarks in D minor.

### 🕐 Practice

**5 minutes**

### ✅ You are ready when

You can construct D minor starting from D **without looking at a scale chart**.

# 🪣 4.9.3 — D Minor vs F Major

This is a **very important Level 4 concept**.

### 🎯 Objective

Understand **relative major and minor**.

D minor and F major contain exactly the same notes.

### F Major

**F – G – A – B♭ – C – D – E – F**

### D Natural Minor

**D – E – F – G – A – B♭ – C – D**

Same notes.

Different starting point.

### 🧠 The key idea

**F Major → starts on F**

**D Minor → starts on D**

Therefore:

> **D minor is the relative minor of F major.**

And:

> **F major is the relative major of D minor.**

### 🎹 Practice exercise

Play:

**F → G → A → B♭ → C → D → E → F**

Then immediately:

**D → E → F → G → A → B♭ → C → D**

Listen carefully.

The notes are the same, but the **tonal center feels different**.

### 🕐 Practice

**5 minutes**

### ✅ You are ready when

Someone says **F major**, and you can immediately think:

**"Its relative minor is D minor."**

And vice versa.

# 🪣 4.9.4 — RH D Minor

Now we start **actual keyboard playing**. 🎹

### 🎯 Objective

Play D natural minor with your **right hand** smoothly.

### RH fingering

Use:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 1 – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5**

Notes:

| Note   | D | E | F | G | A | B♭ | C | D |
| ------ | - | - | - | - | - | -- | - | - |
| Finger | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 3  | 4 | 5 |

So:

**D(1) – E(2) – F(3) – G(1) – A(2) – B♭(3) – C(4) – D(5)**

### 🚶 Ascending

Play slowly:

**D1 – E2 – F3**

Then tuck the thumb underneath:

**G1 – A2 – B♭3 – C4 – D5**

Don't rush the thumb movement.

### 🔙 Descending

Reverse:

**D5 – C4 – B♭3 – A2 – G1 – F3 – E2 – D1**

### 🎯 Important focus

Your biggest technical point is:

**F(3) → G(1)**

The thumb needs to move underneath smoothly.

### 🕐 Practice routine

**5 slow repetitions ascending**

**5 slow repetitions descending**

Then:

**5 times ascending + descending**

Start around **♩ = 50–60 BPM** if you're using a metronome.

### ✅ You are ready when

You can play:

**D–E–F–G–A–B♭–C–D**

and back down without stopping or looking at your fingers.

# 🪣 4.9.5 — LH D Minor

Now train the **left hand independently**.

### 🎯 Objective

Build left-hand control and learn the different fingering pattern.

### LH fingering

Use:

**5 – 4 – 3 – 2 – 1 – 3 – 2 – 1**

Notes:

| Note   | D | E | F | G | A | B♭ | C | D |
| ------ | - | - | - | - | - | -- | - | - |
| Finger | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 3  | 2 | 1 |

So:

**D5 – E4 – F3 – G2 – A1 – B♭3 – C2 – D1**

### 🚶 Ascending

Practice:

**D5 → E4 → F3 → G2 → A1**

Then cross:

**B♭3 → C2 → D1**

The important movement is:

**A(1) → B♭(3)**

### 🔙 Descending

**D1 → C2 → B♭3 → A1 → G2 → F3 → E4 → D5**

### 🧠 Don't try to play fast

Your left hand is probably less comfortable than your right hand.

So prioritize:

**Accuracy → relaxation → consistency → speed**

### 🕐 Practice

* 5× ascending
* 5× descending
* 5× complete scale

Start slowly around **♩ = 50–60 BPM**.

### ✅ You are ready when

Your LH can play D minor without:

* wrong notes
* accidental tension
* stopping at the thumb crossing
* looking constantly at your hand

# 🪣 4.9.6 — Hands Together

🔥 **This is the final goal of 4.9.**

Now combine both hands.

### 🎯 Objective

Play D minor with RH + LH together while maintaining equal rhythm.

Your hands begin on **D**.

### Ascending

Both hands play:

**D → E → F → G → A → B♭ → C → D**

### Descending

Both hands play:

**D → C → B♭ → A → G → F → E → D**

But remember:

**RH fingering**

**1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5**

**LH fingering**

**5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1**

### 🧩 Three-stage method

#### Stage 1 — Very slow

Play one note at a time:

**D → E → F → G → A → B♭ → C → D**

Stop if necessary.

Do **not** worry about speed.

---

#### Stage 2 — Continuous

Play without stopping:

**D E F G A B♭ C D**

Then:

**D C B♭ A G F E D**

Try to keep both hands synchronized.

---

#### Stage 3 — Metronome

Set:

**♩ = 50 BPM**

Play one note per beat.

Then gradually move:

**50 → 60 → 70 → 80 BPM**

Only increase the tempo when you can play accurately.
