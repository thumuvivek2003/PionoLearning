# 🎹 4.3.1 — G Major Notes

### Notes

**G – A – B – C – D – E – F# – G**

There are **7 different notes + octave G**.

On the keyboard:

**G → A → B → C → D → E → F# → G**

The important thing is that **F# is the only black key**.

# 🎹 4.3.2 — F# Recognition

This is one of the **most important parts** of G Major.

You need to understand:

> **Why F# and not F?**

### Start with the major-scale formula

**W – W – H – W – W – W – H**

For G Major:

| Step   | Notes | Distance |
| ------ | ----- | -------- |
| G → A  | Whole | W        |
| A → B  | Whole | W        |
| B → C  | Half  | H        |
| C → D  | Whole | W        |
| D → E  | Whole | W        |
| E → F# | Whole | W        |
| F# → G | Half  | H        |

Notice:

**E → F = Half step**

But we need:

**E → F# = Whole step**

Therefore **F# is required**.

### 🧠 Very important concept

If you played:

**G A B C D E F G**

you would NOT have a G Major scale.

You would have:

**G A B C D E F G**

which does not satisfy the major-scale formula.

The correct version is:

**G A B C D E F# G**

### 🎯 Recognition drill

Randomly point to:

* F
* F#
* G
* E
* F#
* A
* F

Whenever you see **F#**, say:

> **“G Major uses F#.”**

Do this for **2–3 minutes**.

### ⏱️ Suggested time

**5 minutes**

# 🎹 4.3.3 — G Major Formula

Now we move from **memorization → understanding**.

Your formula is:

**W – W – H – W – W – W – H**

Start at G.

### Build it yourself

**G**

⬇ Whole step

**A**

⬇ Whole step

**B**

⬇ Half step

**C**

⬇ Whole step

**D**

⬇ Whole step

**E**

⬇ Whole step

**F#**

⬇ Half step

**G**

Therefore:

**G – A – B – C – D – E – F# – G**

### 🧠 Keyboard exercise

Don't look at a written scale.

Start on **G**.

Ask yourself:

> Whole step?

→ A

> Whole step?

→ B

> Half step?

→ C

Continue until G.

This is teaching your brain to **construct scales**, rather than memorize them.

### 🎯 Challenge

Start from a random G on the keyboard and build the entire scale without looking at your notes.

If you can do this, you genuinely understand G Major.

### ⏱️ Suggested time

**7–10 minutes**

# 🎹 4.3.4 — RH G Major

Now we actually play the scale.

### Right-hand fingering

For one octave:

**G A B C D E F# G**

Use:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 1 – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5**

Where:

* 1 = Thumb
* 2 = Index
* 3 = Middle
* 4 = Ring
* 5 = Little finger

So:

**G(1) – A(2) – B(3) – C(1) – D(2) – E(3) – F#(4) – G(5)**

### 🎯 First exercise — extremely slow

Play:

**G A B C | D E F# G**

Don't worry about speed.

Your priorities:

1. Correct notes
2. Correct fingering
3. Smooth thumb movement
4. Relaxed hand

### 🎯 Second exercise — descending

**G F# E D | C B A G**

Fingering:

**5 – 4 – 3 – 2 – 1 – 3 – 2 – 1**

### 🎯 Third exercise — continuous

Play:

**G A B C D E F# G F# E D C B A G**

Start very slowly.

### 🎯 Fourth exercise — eyes away

Look at your hand less.

Try to feel the keyboard geography.

This is especially useful for your contest preparation.

### ⏱️ Suggested time

**10 minutes**

---

# 🎹 4.3.5 — LH G Major

Now we train the left hand.

### Left-hand fingering

Ascending:

**5 – 4 – 3 – 2 – 1 – 3 – 2 – 1**

So:

**G(5) A(4) B(3) C(2) D(1) E(3) F#(2) G(1)**

Descending:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 1 – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5**

### 🎯 Practice slowly

Ascending:

**G A B C D E F# G**

Then:

**G F# E D C B A G**

Don't try to make the LH as fast as the RH immediately.

The LH usually feels less natural for beginners.

### 🧠 Main objective

Focus on the **thumb crossing**.

Ascending:

**G A B C D → E F# G**

Your thumb moves under after D.

Don't lift your entire hand dramatically.

Keep the movement small and relaxed.

### 🎯 Exercise

Play:

**G A B C D**

Stop.

Reset.

Then:

**E F# G**

Repeat several times.

Then connect the two sections.

### ⏱️ Suggested time

**10 minutes**

---

# 🎹 4.3.6 — Hands Together

🔥 This is where the real coordination begins.

Don't immediately try to play fast.

### Step 1 — Blocked practice

Play both hands slowly:

**RH:** G A B C D E F# G
**LH:** G A B C D E F# G

The hands move at the same time.

### Step 2 — Count

Count:

**1 – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5 – 6 – 7 – 8**

One note per count.

### Step 3 — Ascending only

Play:

**G A B C D E F# G**

with both hands.

Do **5 repetitions**.

### Step 4 — Descending

Play:

**G F# E D C B A G**

with both hands.

Do **5 repetitions**.

### Step 5 — Full scale

Now:

**G A B C D E F# G | G F# E D C B A G**

Do this slowly.

### 🚨 Don't increase speed yet

Your priority is:

**Accuracy → Coordination → Smoothness → Speed**

Not:

**Speed → Mistakes → Frustration 😄**

### 🎯 When can you increase tempo?

When you can play **5 consecutive repetitions without a wrong note or fingering mistake**.

Then increase the tempo slightly.

### ⏱️ Suggested time

**10–15 minutes**

# 🎹 4.3.7 — C → G Comparison

This is extremely useful because it teaches you **how scales are related**.

## C Major

**C – D – E – F – G – A – B – C**

No black keys.

## G Major

**G – A – B – C – D – E – F# – G**

Only **F#** changes.

### Compare them

| C Major | G Major |
| ------- | ------- |
| C       | G       |
| D       | A       |
| E       | B       |
| F       | C       |
| G       | D       |
| A       | E       |
| B       | F#      |
| C       | G       |

The biggest difference to remember:

### C Major

**C D E F G A B C**

### G Major

**G A B C D E F# G**

So your brain should immediately recognize:

> **G Major = C Major pattern shifted to G, with F# instead of F.**

More precisely, don't think of it merely as "shifted C Major"; the important musical relationship is that **G Major has one sharp, F#**.

# 🧠 C → G Recognition Game

Sit at the keyboard.

### Round 1

Play C Major.

Then immediately play G Major.

### Round 2

Say:

**C Major → no sharps**

Then:

**G Major → F#**

### Round 3

Close your eyes.

Think:

**C Major = C D E F G A B C**

Then:

**G Major = G A B C D E F# G**

### Round 4 — Random test

Ask yourself:

> "I'm starting from G. What's my scale?"

Answer:

**G A B C D E F# G**

Then:

> "Which accidental?"

**F#**

Then:

> "Why?"

**Because E → F# must be a whole step and F# → G must be a half step.**

🔥 That's the level of understanding you want.
