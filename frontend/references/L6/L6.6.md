# 6.6.1 — Key Signature Concept

### 🎯 Goal

Understand **what a key signature actually does**.

A key signature is the group of sharps ♯ or flats ♭ placed **immediately after the clef**.

For example:

**Treble clef → ♯**

means every **F** in that piece is normally played as **F♯**.

Think of the key signature as a **permanent instruction** for the piece.

### Practice

#### Step 1 — Look at the staff

Don't play anything yet.

Find:

**Clef → Key Signature → Notes**

Understand that the key signature comes before the actual music notes.

#### Step 2 — Say the rule

Practice saying:

> “The key signature tells me which notes are normally altered throughout the piece.”

#### Step 3 — Keyboard connection

Play:

**F → F♯**

Then say:

> “F is altered to F♯.”

Then play several Fs around the keyboard.

### 🎹 Exercise

Take a simple piece and don't play it.

Just scan every measure and identify:

* What is the clef?
* Is there a key signature?
* How many sharps?
* How many flats?

### ✅ You should be able to

Look at a staff and immediately distinguish:

**Clef | Key Signature | Individual Notes**

# 6.6.2 — Key Signature vs Accidental

This is **very important**.

### 🎯 Goal

Don't confuse:

**Key Signature ♯/♭**

with

**Accidental ♯/♭/♮**

### Key Signature

It applies as the normal rule throughout the music.

Example:

**G Major**

has:

**F♯**

So normally every F is F♯.

### Accidental

An accidental is written **beside a particular note** to temporarily change it.

For example:

G Major normally has F♯.

But if you see:

**F♮**

that particular F becomes natural.

### 🧠 Think like this

**Key signature = default rule**

**Accidental = temporary instruction**

### 🎹 Practice

Take G Major.

Play:

**G A B C D E F♯ G**

Now deliberately play:

**F♮**

Then return to:

**F♯**

Do this several times.

### Exercise

Look at music and ask:

1. What does the key signature tell me?
2. Is this ♯/♭ part of the key signature?
3. Or is it an accidental beside this particular note?

### ✅ Mastery test

You should be able to see:

**♯ at beginning → key signature**

versus

**♯ beside one note → accidental**

without hesitation.

Don't learn these as six unrelated facts.

Learn the **movement of the system**:

**C**
→ add F♯
→ **G**

**G**
→ add C♯
→ **D**

**D**
→ add G♯
→ **A**

And on the flat side:

**C**
→ add B♭
→ **F**

**F**
→ add E♭
→ **B♭**

**B♭**
→ add A♭
→ **E♭**

That way, you're not just memorizing key signatures—you are beginning to **see the logic behind them**, which will make your music reading much faster. 🎹
