Absolutely buddy! 🎹 Since **Bucket 6.8 — Notation → Fingering** is about converting what you see on the staff into the **correct fingers on the keyboard**, we should practice it progressively.

The main goal is **not just memorizing finger numbers**. You want your brain to develop this chain:

**👀 Read note → 🧠 identify pitch → ✋ choose finger → 🎹 play → 👂 listen/check**

Here is a clear practice structure for each of the 7 sessions.

# 🎼 LEVEL 6 — MUSIC READING

## 🎹 Bucket 6.8 — Notation → Fingering

---

## 6.8.1 — Finger Numbers

### 🎯 Objective

Understand and instantly recognize piano finger numbers.

### ✋ Finger numbering

**Right Hand (RH)**

| Finger | Number |
| ------ | -----: |
| Thumb  |      1 |
| Index  |      2 |
| Middle |      3 |
| Ring   |      4 |
| Pinky  |      5 |

**Left Hand (LH)**

| Finger | Number |
| ------ | -----: |
| Pinky  |      5 |
| Ring   |      4 |
| Middle |      3 |
| Index  |      2 |
| Thumb  |      1 |

> Important: **the numbering is the same for both hands**. Finger 1 is always the thumb and finger 5 is always the pinky.

### 🏋️ Practice

**Exercise A — Finger identification**

Place your hands on your lap.

Randomly call:

`1 → 3 → 5 → 2 → 4`

Then immediately move the corresponding finger.

Do this with both hands.

**Exercise B — Keyboard**

Put RH on:

`C D E F G`

and play:

`1 2 3 4 5`

Then LH:

`C D E F G`

but play:

`5 4 3 2 1`

### ⏱️ Practice

**5–7 minutes**

### ✅ Move on when

You can hear/read:

> “RH finger 3”

and immediately know:

> **middle finger**

without thinking.

---

# 6.8.2 — Right-Hand Fingering

### 🎯 Objective

Develop consistent RH fingering for simple melodies.

Start with **five-finger patterns**.

Put RH:

`C D E F G`

with:

`1 2 3 4 5`

### 🏋️ Exercise 1 — Ascending

Play:

`C D E F G`

with:

`1 2 3 4 5`

Then descend:

`G F E D C`

with:

`5 4 3 2 1`

### 🏋️ Exercise 2 — Random patterns

Play:

`C D E D C`

Fingering:

`1 2 3 2 1`

Then:

`C E G E C`

Fingering:

`1 3 5 3 1`

Then:

`G F E D E`

Fingering:

`5 4 3 2 3`

### 🏋️ Exercise 3 — Read + play

Write or use simple notes on the treble staff:

`C D E F G`

Don't look at your fingers first.

Instead:

**See note → identify note → think finger → play.**

### ⏱️ Practice

**10 minutes**

### 🎯 Focus

Don't rush.

Your priority is:

**Correct note + correct finger + steady rhythm**

not speed.

---

# 6.8.3 — Left-Hand Fingering

### 🎯 Objective

Become comfortable reading and fingering notes with LH.

Place LH on:

`C D E F G`

using:

`5 4 3 2 1`

### 🏋️ Exercise 1 — Ascending

`C D E F G`

with:

`5 4 3 2 1`

Descending:

`G F E D C`

with:

`1 2 3 4 5`

### 🏋️ Exercise 2 — Simple patterns

Try:

`C D E D C`

with:

`5 4 3 4 5`

Then:

`C E G E C`

with:

`5 3 1 3 5`

### 🏋️ Exercise 3 — Bass-clef reading

Read simple bass-clef notes and play them using the assigned fingers.

Your mental process should be:

**Bass note → identify pitch → identify finger → play**

### ⚠️ Common beginner problem

Many beginners automatically think:

> "Left hand = fingers go backward."

Instead think:

> **Finger numbers belong to the fingers, not the direction of the hand.**

### ⏱️ Practice

**10 minutes**

---

# 6.8.4 — Five-Finger Positions

### 🎯 Objective

Learn to keep your hand stable while reading and playing five-note patterns.

This is extremely important because it creates the foundation for **melody reading**.

Start with:

**RH**

`C D E F G`

`1 2 3 4 5`

**LH**

`C D E F G`

`5 4 3 2 1`

---

### 🏋️ Exercise 1 — Five-note ladder

RH:

`C D E F G F E D C`

Fingering:

`1 2 3 4 5 4 3 2 1`

LH:

`C D E F G F E D C`

Fingering:

`5 4 3 2 1 2 3 4 5`

---

### 🏋️ Exercise 2 — Skip patterns

RH:

`C E D F E G`

Try:

`1 3 2 4 3 5`

Then reverse it.

---

### 🏋️ Exercise 3 — Pattern recognition

Play patterns such as:

`C D E C`

`D E F D`

`E F G E`

Ask yourself:

> "What is the fingering pattern?"

This develops **pattern recognition**, which is much faster than calculating every finger individually.

### ⏱️ Practice

**10–15 minutes**

### 🎯 Goal

Eventually, when you see:

**C–D–E–F–G**

your brain should automatically associate:

**1–2–3–4–5**

---

# 6.8.5 — Scale Fingering

### 🎯 Objective

Understand that fingering changes when you move beyond a five-finger position.

This is where **thumb-under / finger crossing** becomes important.

Start with the **C major scale**.

### 🎹 Right Hand — C Major

Notes:

`C D E F G A B C`

Fingering:

`1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5`

Notice the important movement:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 1**

The thumb moves underneath after finger 3.

---

### 🎹 Left Hand — C Major

Notes:

`C D E F G A B C`

Fingering:

`5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1`

Here the thumb movement happens after finger 1.

---

### 🏋️ Practice method

Don't immediately play the whole scale quickly.

Break it into chunks.

RH:

`C D E`

`1 2 3`

Then:

`F G A B C`

`1 2 3 4 5`

Then connect them.

Do the same descending.

### 🎯 Reading exercise

Write:

`C D E F G A B C`

Look at the notes and say:

> "1 2 3 — 1 2 3 4 5"

Then play.

### ⏱️ Practice

**15 minutes**

### ⚠️ Important

At this stage, don't try to learn every major/minor scale.

For Bucket 6.8, first become extremely comfortable with:

**C Major fingering**

Then gradually expand.

---

# 6.8.6 — Melody Fingering

### 🎯 Objective

Learn how to select practical fingering when playing an actual melody.

This is where notation finally starts becoming **real music**.

For example, suppose you see:

`C D E G E D C`

You could use:

`1 2 3 5 3 2 1`

That's easy.

But now imagine:

`C D E F G A G F E`

You need to think about **hand movement**.

---

### 🧠 Fingering decision rules

When choosing fingering, ask:

### Rule 1 — Can I keep the hand comfortable?

Don't use awkward fingers just because they technically reach the note.

### Rule 2 — Can I avoid unnecessary hand movement?

If the next notes are close together, keep the hand stable.

### Rule 3 — Does the melody move upward?

You may need to shift or cross fingers.

### Rule 4 — Does the melody move downward?

The reverse movement may be needed.

### Rule 5 — Think ahead

Don't decide the finger only for the current note.

Look at the **next 2–4 notes**.

This is a major skill.

---

### 🏋️ Practice

Take a very easy melody.

For example:

**Mary Had a Little Lamb**

`E D C D E E E`

Try:

`3 2 1 2 3 3 3`

Then:

`D D D`

`2 2 2`

Then:

`E G G`

Choose comfortable fingering.

The important part is:

**READ → PLAN → PLAY**

not simply:

**READ → PLAY**

### ⏱️ Practice

**15 minutes**

# 6.8.7 — Fingering + Reading

## ⭐ The Most Important Session

### 🎯 Objective

Combine:

**Notation + Pitch + Rhythm + Fingering + Keyboard**

This is where Bucket 6.8 becomes practical.

# 🏋️ Main Practice Drill

Take a very short piece containing **5–10 notes**.

### Round 1 — Notes only

Read the notes without playing.

Example:

`C D E F G`

Say:

> C – D – E – F – G

### Round 2 — Fingering

Say:

> 1 – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5

### Round 3 — Rhythm

Tap the rhythm while saying the notes.

### Round 4 — Play slowly

Now play.

### Round 5 — Eyes on notation

Try not to stare at your fingers.

### Round 6 — Repeat

Play again while maintaining:

**correct notes + correct fingers + correct rhythm**

# 🚦 Difficulty Progression

For every new exercise, use this progression:

### 🟢 Level A — Very Slow

**50–60 BPM**

Focus entirely on accuracy.

### 🟡 Level B — Comfortable

**60–80 BPM**

Maintain steady rhythm.

### 🔵 Level C — Performance

Increase gradually only if your fingering remains clean.

**Never increase speed by sacrificing fingering accuracy.**

# 🏆 What You Should Be Able To Do After 6.8

By the time you've properly completed this bucket, you should be able to look at simple notation and think:

> **"I know what note this is, I know where it is on the keyboard, I know which finger should play it, and I can play it without stopping."**

That's the real target.

And one particularly important habit for your contest preparation:

### 🎼 Don't memorize fingering separately from the music.

Instead train:

**NOTE → FINGER → KEY**

as one single association.

That will make your **music-reading speed, keyboard geography, and playing fluency** reinforce each other rather than becoming three separate skills.
