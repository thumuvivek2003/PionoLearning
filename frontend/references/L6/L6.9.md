# 🎼 LEVEL 6 — MUSIC READING

## 👀 Bucket 6.9 — Sight Reading

### 1. One-Note Reading

**Goal:** Build instant note recognition.

**What to do:**

* Choose 5–10 notes from the staff.
* Look at one note.
* Identify its letter name.
* Find the corresponding key.
* Play it immediately.
* Repeat with random notes.

**Example:**

`Treble G → identify G → locate G → play G`

Then gradually increase the speed.

**Practice progression:**

* Stage 1: Note → Name
* Stage 2: Note → Name → Key
* Stage 3: Note → Key directly
* Stage 4: Random notes with no hesitation

**Target:** You should eventually recognize common notes **without consciously counting every line and space**.

⏱️ **Practice:** 5 minutes

---

### 2. Two-Note Patterns

**Goal:** Train your eyes to read more than one note at once.

Instead of reading:

> Note → stop → play → Note → stop → play

try:

> **See both notes → understand the movement → play**

Start with simple patterns:

* C → D
* D → E
* E → D
* C → E
* E → C
* G → F
* C → G

Then introduce different rhythms.

**Practice idea:**

Look at:

`C D | D E | E F | F G`

Try playing without stopping between notes.

Then reverse the direction.

**Target:** Your eyes begin anticipating the **next note** while your fingers are playing the current note.

⏱️ **Practice:** 5 minutes

---

### 3. Three-Note Patterns

**Goal:** Develop short-term visual memory and reading fluency.

Now read groups of three notes rather than individual notes.

Examples:

* C–D–E
* E–D–C
* C–E–G
* G–E–C
* D–E–F
* F–E–D

Then introduce stepwise melodies:

`C D E D C`

Instead of thinking:

> C... D... E... D... C

think:

> **C-D-E-D-C = one musical shape**

### Important exercise

Look at the entire 3-note group **before playing**.

Then play it.

This teaches your eyes to process **patterns**, not isolated symbols.

⏱️ **Practice:** 5 minutes

---

# 4. Simple Melodies

**Goal:** Apply note recognition to actual music.

Now move from exercises into tiny melodies.

Start with melodies containing:

* 3–5 different notes
* mostly stepwise movement
* simple rhythms
* no large jumps
* one hand at a time

For example:

`C D E | E D C | C D E | D C`

Before playing:

1. Identify the starting note.
2. Look for repeated notes.
3. Look for ascending patterns.
4. Look for descending patterns.
5. Notice large jumps.
6. Check the rhythm.
7. Then play.

### Very important:

**Do not memorize the melody first.**

Your objective is to make your brain process:

**notation → keyboard**

rather than:

**notation → memorize → play**

⏱️ **Practice:** 10 minutes

---

# 5. Treble Sight Reading

🎹 **Right Hand Focus**

**Goal:** Become comfortable reading the upper staff.

Start with very simple right-hand exercises.

Practice:

* Treble staff notes
* Middle C
* D–E–F–G–A
* Stepwise melodies
* Small intervals
* Simple rhythms

### Exercise structure

**Round 1**

Read slowly with no metronome.

**Round 2**

Play the same material slightly faster.

**Round 3**

Take a completely NEW exercise.

This is important.

If you repeatedly practice the same piece, you may be practicing **memory**, not sight reading.

### Target

Your eyes should gradually move from:

**note → key**

toward:

**musical pattern → hand movement**

⏱️ **Practice:** 10 minutes

---

# 6. Bass Sight Reading

🎹 **Left Hand Focus**

**Goal:** Become comfortable reading the lower staff.

This is particularly important because beginners often become much slower with bass clef.

Start with:

* Bass staff landmarks
* Middle C
* C–D–E–F–G around the bass region
* Simple stepwise movement
* Repeated notes
* Small intervals

### Exercise

Take a very easy bass-clef melody.

Read:

**one measure → play**

Then:

**two measures → play**

Then:

**four measures → play**

Gradually increase the visual chunk.

### Don't do this:

> Count every line and space from scratch.

Instead, develop **landmark recognition**.

⏱️ **Practice:** 10 minutes

---

# 7. Hands Separately

**Goal:** Make each hand independently comfortable with reading.

Now alternate between:

### Right Hand

Read a completely new melody.

### Left Hand

Read another completely new melody.

Then switch immediately.

The important part is that you **don't prepare the piece for several minutes**.

Give yourself perhaps **10–15 seconds to inspect it**, then begin.

### Practice format

**RH**

* 2 minutes easy
* 2 minutes moderate

**LH**

* 2 minutes easy
* 2 minutes moderate

Then:

**RH + LH alternating**

* 4 minutes

This develops flexibility.

⏱️ **Practice:** 10 minutes

---

# 8. Hands Together

🔥 **This is a major milestone.**

**Goal:** Read both staffs simultaneously.

Start extremely easy.

Don't choose difficult music just because you can technically play it.

Your sight-reading piece should be **easier than your normal practice repertoire**.

### Step-by-step

**Level 1**

Both hands play very simple notes.

**Level 2**

Both hands move stepwise.

**Level 3**

Different rhythms between hands.

**Level 4**

Simple intervals/chords in one hand.

**Level 5**

Short two-staff melodies.

### Eye strategy

Don't constantly look down at your fingers.

Try:

**Look at notation → hands move → eyes return to notation**

Your eyes should gradually stay ahead of your fingers.

⏱️ **Practice:** 10 minutes

---

# 9. No-Stopping Practice

🔥🔥 **Extremely important for your contest.**

This trains you not to collapse when you make a mistake.

The rule is:

> **Once you start, you don't stop.**

Suppose you play:

`C D E F G`

and accidentally play the wrong note on F.

Don't stop.

Continue:

`C D E F❌ G A B...`

The objective isn't perfect accuracy.

The objective is:

**KEEP THE MUSIC MOVING.**

### Exercise

Take a very easy, unfamiliar piece.

Set a comfortable tempo.

Start.

If you make a mistake:

❌ Don't stop
❌ Don't restart
❌ Don't go back
❌ Don't stare at the wrong note

Instead:

✅ Keep going
✅ Find the next note
✅ Maintain the pulse

### Contest mindset

Think:

> **"One mistake is one mistake. Stopping turns it into a disaster."**

⏱️ **Practice:** 5–10 minutes

---

# 10. Metronome Reading

🥁 **Goal:** Combine reading + rhythm + keyboard control.

This is the final integration.

Take an easy sight-reading exercise.

Set a slow metronome.

For example:

**♩ = 50 BPM**

Then play while maintaining the pulse.

Your priorities should be:

1. **Keep the beat**
2. **Keep reading**
3. **Keep playing**
4. **Maintain reasonable accuracy**

Don't immediately increase the tempo.

### Progression

**Day 1**

♩ = 40

**Then**

♩ = 50

**Then**

♩ = 60

**Then**

♩ = 70

Only increase the tempo when you can maintain continuity.

### Important rule

If the music becomes so difficult that you constantly stop:

**Lower the tempo.**

Don't fight the metronome.

⏱️ **Practice:** 10 minutes
